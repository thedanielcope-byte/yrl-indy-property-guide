Best Indianapolis Areas for Young Professionals

Ranked for a short commute to downtown jobs, walkable nightlife and dining, and homes that rent and resell well — with the honest tradeoffs.

For young professionals, the right neighborhood usually comes down to four things: how fast you can reach downtown jobs, whether you can walk to dinner and a night out, what your budget actually buys, and how easily the place rents or resells when your plans change. Indianapolis is unusual in offering several areas that deliver on all four — which is exactly why the choice feels hard.

Below are the eight we most often recommend to buyers early in their careers — a mix of close-in, walkable Marion County neighborhoods and newer, more affordable suburbs — ranked, with the tradeoffs stated plainly.

How We Chose These

These picks come from the data we publish on every one of our 85 Central Indiana community pages — typical price range, drive time to downtown Indianapolis, walkability, and dining and nightlife nearby — combined with what buyers early in their careers actually ask us for. We weighted commute and walkability first, then price accessibility, then how well each area tends to rent and resell.

We've noted the real tradeoffs, including where an area is a poor fit. Nothing here is sponsored, and there is no ranking fee — we're a local MIBOR brokerage, not a directory.

1. Fountain Square — Best Overall

Best for: young professionals who want walkable nightlife and dining a few minutes from downtown jobs.

Fountain Square packs murals, music venues, independent restaurants and a couple of vintage duckpin-bowling bars into a genuinely walkable district about five minutes from the downtown job core. Homes start around the $200s and run to $500s+, and steady demand makes it one of the easier close-in neighborhoods to rent out or resell later.

The tradeoff: the most desirable blocks have already appreciated, so you're buying in later than the earliest buyers did, and parking gets tight on weekend nights.

2. Broad Ripple — Best for Nightlife & Dining

Best for: young professionals whose weekends revolve around bars, restaurants and live music.

Broad Ripple is the metro's best-known nightlife-and-dining village, walkable along the canal and the Monon Trail, with homes from the $200s into the $600s+. It's roughly 15 minutes to downtown and rents extremely well, which makes it a favorite for buyers who may lease the place out down the road.

The tradeoff: weekend-night crowds and noise near the village core, so choose your block with that in mind.

3. Mass Ave — Best for Car-Light Downtown Living

Best for: young professionals who work downtown and want to walk or bike instead of commute.

Mass Ave is downtown's walkable arts corridor — theaters, galleries, restaurants and stylish lofts and condos, with homes generally $200s–$500s+. If you work downtown, this is as close to car-optional as Indianapolis gets, and the Cultural Trail links you to the rest of the core.

The tradeoff: inventory is limited and leans toward condos and lofts, so single-family options are scarce and any condo or HOA fees factor into the budget.

4. Fishers — Best Suburb for Young Professionals

Best for: young professionals who want a newer home and suburban space without giving up a night out.

If you'd rather have new construction and a shorter maintenance list, Fishers pairs a growing base of tech and corporate employers with the walkable Nickel Plate District for dining and events. Homes typically run $300s–$600s, and it's about 20 minutes downtown via I-69.

The tradeoff: it's a car-dependent, master-planned suburb — great for space and newness, less so if you want to step out your door straight into nightlife.

5. Fletcher Place — Best Near-Downtown Alternative

Best for: young professionals who want Fountain Square's walkability at a calmer, close-in address.

Fletcher Place sits between Fountain Square and downtown, compact and walkable to both, with a well-regarded dining corridor of its own. Homes generally run $250s–$500s, and the Cultural Trail puts downtown jobs within an easy walk or bike.

The tradeoff: it's small with limited inventory, and like most near-downtown blocks the housing is older, so budget for mechanicals.

6. Speedway — Best Value

Best for: young professionals who want the lowest entry price and a home that rents well.

Speedway is its own town inside Marion County, with its own schools and a Main Street that has seen real investment in recent years. Homes run $150s–$280s about 15 minutes from downtown — the most affordable pick here, and a strong one for rentability given the year-round draw around the motor speedway.

The tradeoff: older housing stock, so factor in updates, and it's more everyday-town than nightlife destination.

7. Greenwood — Best South-Side Suburb

Best for: young professionals working or living on the south side who want amenities and a short drive.

Greenwood offers a roughly 15-minute commute via I-65, every major retailer along US-31, and a revitalized Old Town with restaurants and a public market. Homes run $250s–$450s, and steady demand supports resale.

The tradeoff: the US-31 retail corridor is convenient but congested at peak, and it's suburban rather than walkable outside Old Town.

8. Whitestown — Best New Construction

Best for: young professionals who want a brand-new home near west- and northwest-side job centers.

One of the fastest-growing towns in the state, Whitestown is built around the Anson development, which mixes new housing with shops, restaurants and major employers. Newer homes generally start in the $300s, and I-65 access makes the west and northwest job corridors easy.

The tradeoff: it's about 25 minutes from downtown and thoroughly car-dependent, and you're paying more for newness than you would for an older, close-in home.

How to Choose

Start with where you actually work and how you want to get there. If you can walk or bike to a downtown job, a place like Mass Ave or Fountain Square pays for itself in time and car costs; if you want a newer home and don't mind driving, Fishers or Whitestown make more sense. Then weigh rentability honestly — if there's any chance you'll move for work in a few years, the close-in, walkable neighborhoods tend to lease and resell fastest. Finally, tour on a weekend night as well as a weekday, because nightlife-adjacent blocks feel very different after dark.

Frequently Asked Questions — Best Areas for Young Professionals

What is the best Indianapolis suburb for young professionals?

For a walkable, close-in lifestyle, Fountain Square is the strongest all-round pick — about five minutes from downtown with nightlife and dining at the door and homes from the $200s. If you'd rather have a newer suburban home, Fishers is the standout, pairing growing employers with the walkable Nickel Plate District. Broad Ripple leads for nightlife, and Speedway is the best value from the $150s.

Where should young professionals live near Indianapolis?

It depends on how you want to commute. If you work downtown and want to walk or bike, look at Mass Ave, Fountain Square or Fletcher Place. If you'd rather drive from a newer home, Fishers, Greenwood and Whitestown offer more space and new construction. Broad Ripple splits the difference with a lively village about 15 minutes out.

What are the most walkable areas near Indianapolis?

Mass Ave, Fountain Square, Fletcher Place and Broad Ripple are the most walkable, each built around a compact commercial strip you can reach on foot. Mass Ave and Fletcher Place also put downtown jobs within walking or biking distance via the Cultural Trail. Among the suburbs, Fishers' Nickel Plate District and Greenwood's Old Town are the most walkable pockets.

Are homes near downtown Indianapolis a good investment for young professionals?

Close-in, walkable neighborhoods like Fountain Square, Broad Ripple and Fletcher Place tend to rent and resell well because demand for walkable living is strong, which matters if you might relocate for work later. Just remember most of this housing is older, so a thorough inspection of the furnace, roof, wiring and foundation is essential, and any purchase should pencil out on today's numbers rather than assumed appreciation.
