Most Affordable Indianapolis Suburbs

The lowest-price towns around Indianapolis that are still genuinely livable — ranked by value, with the honest tradeoff on commute, schools and amenities for each.

Central Indiana is one of the more affordable metros in the country, and you don't have to leave it to find real value — several towns within half an hour of downtown Indianapolis still have homes starting in the $150s and $160s. "Affordable" here doesn't mean run-down; it usually means an older, smaller-lot housing stock, a slightly longer drive, or fewer big-box amenities than you'd get in Carmel or Fishers.

Below are the eight towns we most often point value-focused buyers toward, ranked by how much you actually get for the lower price. We've been honest about the tradeoff on every one — because with affordability, the tradeoff is the whole story. If you're buying at the entry level, it's also worth pairing this list with our first-time buyer and down payment assistance pages to stretch your budget further.

How We Chose These

We ranked on entry price first, then on value-for-money — how little you give up to hit that lower price. Towns near the top pair a low price floor with a short commute or a real downtown; the ones lower down are cheaper still, or start a little higher, but ask for a longer drive or fewer amenities in return. Where a town's school district is a genuine draw at the price, we've said so. Prices, districts and drive times come from the data we publish on every one of our 85 Central Indiana community pages.

We've noted the real tradeoffs, including where a town is a poor fit. Nothing here is sponsored, and there is no ranking fee — we're a local MIBOR brokerage, not a directory.

1. Beech Grove — Best Value Overall

Best for: buyers who want the lowest entry price without a long commute

Nothing else on this list matches the combination: homes from the $150s and roughly 12 minutes to downtown. Beech Grove is an "excluded city" inside Marion County with its own Beech Grove City Schools district, separate from Indianapolis Public Schools, which is a real draw at this price. See homes for sale in Beech Grove →

The tradeoff: it's a small, established city — expect older bungalows and modest ranches on smaller lots, with little new construction, so budget for updates.

2. Lawrence — Best Close-In Northeast Value

Best for: buyers who want an established, amenity-rich area close to the city

Lawrence sits on the northeast side about 20 minutes from downtown, served by MSD of Lawrence Township, with homes from the $160s into the $320s. Fort Harrison State Park and the redeveloping Fort Ben area give it more green space and character than most value picks. See how it stacks up against a premium neighbor in our Lawrence vs Fishers comparison, or browse Lawrence listings →

The tradeoff: it's a large, varied area, so pricing and feel swing block to block — confirm the specific neighborhood and attendance zone before you commit.

3. Shelbyville — Rock-Bottom Entry With a Real Downtown

Best for: buyers who want the lowest possible price and a genuine downtown, and can accept a 25-minute drive

Shelbyville pairs one of the lowest price floors in the metro — from the $150s — with a classic courthouse-square downtown and its own Shelbyville Central Schools. It's about 25 minutes down I-74, so the commute stays workable. See homes for sale in Shelbyville →

The tradeoff: it's a small city rather than a suburb, so you'll drive to Greenwood or the south side for big-box shopping and a wider dining scene.

4. Franklin — Best Real Downtown Under Budget

Best for: buyers who want authentic small-city character and respected schools without Center Grove prices

Franklin is the best character-per-dollar option here: a walkable historic square, Franklin College, and Franklin Community Schools, with homes from the $180s. It's the Johnson County seat, about 30 minutes south via I-65. Compare it to its pricier neighbor in our Greenwood vs Franklin guide, or see Franklin listings →

The tradeoff: the roughly 30-minute commute is longer than closer-in Greenwood, and downtown-area homes are often older and need work.

5. Greenfield — Hancock County Value With Character

Best for: buyers who want a courthouse-square town on the east side

The Hancock County seat and hometown of poet James Whitcomb Riley, Greenfield offers a genuine downtown and Greenfield-Central Community Schools, with homes from the $200s into the $400s and beyond. It's about 30 minutes east along I-70 and US-40. See homes for sale in Greenfield →

The tradeoff: the entry price sits above the $150s towns, and the US-40 corridor can be slow at peak times.

6. Whiteland — Best Value Plus Schools

Best for: buyers who want respected schools and newer homes on a tighter budget

Whiteland is the pick when schools matter but budget is tight. It's in the well-regarded Clark-Pleasant Community Schools district just east of Greenwood, with direct I-65 access and a 25-minute commute, and a lot of relatively affordable newer construction from the $250s. See Greenwood vs Whiteland, or browse Whiteland listings →

The tradeoff: the entry price is higher than the $150s–$160s towns, and it's growing fast, so some subdivisions are still building out their amenities.

7. Mooresville — Southwest-Side Value

Best for: buyers commuting southwest or toward the airport

Mooresville keeps a small-town feel on the southwest side, served by the Mooresville Consolidated School Corporation, with homes from the $250s into the $450s and beyond, about 25 minutes out via SR-67. It's a practical value base for airport-area or west-side jobs. Compare it in our Mooresville vs Greenwood guide, or see Mooresville listings →

The tradeoff: fewer everyday amenities than Greenwood, and the SR-67 corridor is the main route in and out.

8. Anderson — The Cheapest Floor in the Metro

Best for: buyers who want the absolute lowest prices and can work hybrid or remote

Anderson has some of the lowest home prices in Central Indiana, typically from the $150s to the $250s, served by the Anderson Community School Corporation in Madison County. If your budget is the hard constraint, your dollar goes furthest here. See homes for sale in Anderson →

The tradeoff: the commute — roughly 35 to 45 minutes northeast up I-69 — makes a daily downtown drive tough, so it fits best for hybrid, remote, or Anderson-area jobs.

How to Choose

Start with the commute math, not the sticker price — a longer daily drive is the most common hidden cost of an affordable suburb, and the towns here run from about 12 minutes (Beech Grove) to 45 (Anderson). Then remember that a lower price usually buys an older home, so leave room in the budget for updates, and check the attendance zone for any specific address rather than trusting the town name. Finally, if you're at the entry level, our down payment assistance and first-time buyer pages can stretch what you qualify for further than most buyers expect.

Frequently Asked Questions — Most Affordable Indianapolis Suburbs

What is the most affordable Indianapolis suburb?

Beech Grove is the best value close in, with homes from the $150s, a roughly 12-minute drive downtown, and its own Beech Grove City Schools district. Shelbyville and Anderson also start around the $150s, and Lawrence around the $160s, though each asks for a longer commute in return.

What is the cheapest place to live near Indianapolis with good schools?

Whiteland is a strong pick, with homes from the $250s in the well-regarded Clark-Pleasant Community Schools district and direct I-65 access. Franklin is cheaper, from the $180s with Franklin Community Schools, and Lawrence starts around the $160s in MSD of Lawrence Township. Always confirm the attendance zone for the specific address, since boundaries do not follow town lines.

How much do homes cost in the affordable Indianapolis suburbs?

Entry-level homes commonly start in the $150s to $200s in towns like Beech Grove, Shelbyville, Anderson and Lawrence, rising into the $300s and $400s for newer or larger properties. Franklin starts around the $180s, while Whiteland and Mooresville generally begin in the $250s. These are ranges only, and exact prices move with the market and a home's condition.

Does an affordable suburb mean a worse commute?

Not always. Beech Grove is about 12 minutes from downtown and Lawrence about 20. But the very lowest price floors, like Anderson, come with a 35 to 45 minute drive, and towns such as Franklin and Greenfield sit around 30 minutes. Weigh the daily commute against the price savings before you decide.
