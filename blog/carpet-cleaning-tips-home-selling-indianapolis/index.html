Simple Carpet Cleaning Tips Get Floors Show-Ready Before You List

Clean, fresh carpet is one of the cheapest ways to help a home show its best.

Home Prep Staging Central Indiana

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Selling a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~5 min read

Carpet is making a comeback, especially in bedrooms where soft, quiet floors just feel like home. But carpet also shows its age fast, and few things turn a buyer off quicker than stains or a musty smell underfoot. The good news is you can freshen most carpet yourself with things you already have. Here are simple tricks to get your floors show-ready, and if a stain won't budge, our local vendor directory can connect you with a trusted cleaning pro.

For Everyday Dirt, Crumbs, and Pet Hair

Start with the easy stuff. A basic lint roller is surprisingly good at lifting crumbs, glitter, spilled flour or rice, and fine debris that a vacuum skips over, which is handy for quick touch-ups before a showing.

For stubborn pet hair woven into the fibers, run a damp squeegee across the carpet. The rubber edge gathers hair into piles you can pick up by hand. It's slower than a vacuum but far more thorough on the fur that pets leave behind.

The Right Way to Tackle Stains

When something spills, blot, don't rub. Rubbing pushes the stain deeper and spreads it wider into the fibers. Press a clean cloth straight down to soak up as much as you can, working from the outside of the spill toward the center.

For a gentle DIY cleaner, reach for common household items like white vinegar, a little dish soap in water, or hydrogen peroxide for lighter carpets. Always test on a hidden spot first. A quick pass of warm air from a blow dryer can also loosen a set-in stain so it lifts more easily.

Freshen and Deodorize

Carpets hold onto smells from pets, cooking, and everyday life. An easy homemade deodorizer is a mix of baking soda with a few drops of essential oil, and some folks add a little borax. Sprinkle it over the carpet, let it sit for several minutes, then vacuum it up.

Shaving cream is another budget-friendly trick for small spots, working a bit like a spot cleaner to lift grime and leave things smelling fresh. For a whole house before listing, though, a professional deep clean is often worth every penny.

Why Fresh Carpet Matters When You Sell

Buyers notice flooring the moment they walk in, and clean carpet signals a home that's been cared for. Stains, matting, or odor make people wonder what else has been neglected, even when the rest of the house is spotless.

Before photos and showings, give carpets a thorough vacuum, treat any visible stains, and deodorize. If they're truly worn, ask your agent whether a professional cleaning or replacement makes sense for your market; sometimes it's the difference between a quick sale and a home that lingers. Frequently Asked Questions

Should I replace carpet before selling my home?

Not always. A deep professional cleaning often refreshes carpet enough to show well. Replace it only when it's badly worn or stained, and ask your agent whether the cost pays off in your market.

How do I get pet smells out of carpet?

Sprinkle a baking-soda-based deodorizer, let it sit, then vacuum. For set-in pet odors, a professional cleaning is usually the most reliable fix before you list.

What's the best homemade carpet stain remover?

Common household items work well: white vinegar, a little dish soap in water, or hydrogen peroxide on lighter carpets. Always blot instead of rubbing, and test on a hidden area first.

Does clean carpet really help a home sell?

Yes. Buyers read clean floors as a sign the whole home is well maintained, while stains and odors raise doubts. Fresh carpet is one of the most affordable ways to show a home well.
