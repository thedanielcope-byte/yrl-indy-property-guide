Contingencies in an Indiana Purchase Agreement, Explained

Financing, inspection, appraisal, title — the conditions in your contract are what protect your money. Here is how contingencies work for buyers in Central Indiana.

Buying a Home 7 min read By Daniel Cope

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Buying a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~7 min read

When you sign a purchase agreement to buy a home, you are not just naming a price — you are agreeing to a set of conditions that have to be met before the deal becomes final. Those conditions are called contingencies, and understanding purchase agreement contingencies in Indiana is one of the most important parts of protecting yourself as a buyer. They are the safety valves that let you walk away — with your deposit intact — if something important falls through. Here is a plain-English look at how they work in Central Indiana, and why they matter whether the market favors buyers or sellers.

What Is a Contingency?

A contingency is a condition written into your purchase agreement that must be satisfied before you are obligated to close. Think of it as an if-then clause: if a certain condition is not met, then you have the right to renegotiate or cancel the contract without penalty. Contingencies come with deadlines, and both buyer and seller agree to them when they sign.

Contingencies are closely tied to your earnest money — the good-faith deposit you put down when your offer is accepted. When your contract is properly written and you exercise a contingency within its deadline, that deposit is typically returned to you. Miss the deadline or waive the contingency, however, and your earnest money can be at risk. That is why the details and timelines matter so much.

The Common Contingencies in an Indiana Purchase Agreement

Indiana purchase agreements can include several contingencies. These are the ones our buyers encounter most often:

  • Financing and appraisal. A financing contingency gives you an out if your mortgage falls through. It usually pairs with an appraisal condition: if the lender’s appraisal comes in below the agreed price, you can renegotiate or cancel rather than cover the gap in cash.
  • Inspection. An inspection contingency lets you hire a professional for a home inspection and then request repairs, negotiate credits, or walk away if the report reveals serious problems.
  • Sale of home. If you need to sell your current house to buy the next one, a sale-of-home contingency ties the purchase to that sale closing first.
  • Title. A title contingency ensures the seller can convey clear, marketable title. If a lien, easement, or ownership issue surfaces, it must be resolved before you are required to close.

How Contingencies Protect a Buyer

Each contingency exists to protect a specific part of your investment. The financing and appraisal conditions protect your wallet — they keep you from being forced to buy a home you cannot fund or to overpay beyond what the property is worth. The inspection contingency protects you from inheriting expensive, hidden defects. The title contingency protects your legal ownership, and a sale-of-home contingency protects you from owning two houses at once.

Together, contingencies give you defined windows to do your due diligence — to verify the home is sound, financeable, and legally clean — before your money is truly committed. Used correctly, they are the single biggest reason a buyer can proceed with confidence rather than crossing their fingers and hoping everything works out.

What Waiving a Contingency Means in a Competitive Offer

In a hot market, buyers sometimes waive contingencies to make their offers more attractive to sellers. Waiving a contingency means giving up that protection — and the leverage that comes with it. Waive the inspection, and you accept the home’s condition without a professional’s eyes on it. Waive the appraisal, and you may be agreeing to make up any shortfall between the appraised value and the purchase price out of pocket.

Waiving can absolutely help you win a home, but it shifts risk squarely onto you. It also changes how a listing moves through the process — you will often see homes marked contingent versus pending as they clear these conditions. Before you drop any protection to strengthen an offer, it is worth understanding exactly what you are trading away, because the money at stake can be substantial.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the most common contingencies in an Indiana purchase agreement?

The most common are financing and appraisal, inspection, title, and — when a buyer must sell first — a sale-of-home contingency. Each one gives the buyer a defined way to renegotiate or exit if a condition is not met.

Can I get my earnest money back if I use a contingency?

Usually yes. If your contract is properly written and you exercise a valid contingency within its deadline, your earnest money is typically returned. Missing the deadline or waiving the contingency can put that deposit at risk.

Is it a good idea to waive contingencies to win a bidding war?

It can help your offer stand out, but it shifts significant risk onto you. Waiving the inspection or appraisal, for example, can be costly. Weigh each one carefully with your agent before giving up a protection.

Does this article count as legal advice?

No. This is general educational information about how contingencies work in Central Indiana. For advice on your specific contract, confirm the details with your agent and, where appropriate, a licensed Indiana real estate attorney.
