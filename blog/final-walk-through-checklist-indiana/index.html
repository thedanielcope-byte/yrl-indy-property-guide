The Final Walk-Through Checklist for Indiana Buyers

The final walk-through is your last look before the keys are yours. Here is exactly what to check, and what to do if something is not right.

Buying a Home Central Indiana By Daniel Cope

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Buying a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

You are days from owning your new home, and there is one last step many Central Indiana buyers underestimate: the final walk-through. This is the short visit, usually a day or two before closing, where you confirm the home is in the condition you agreed to buy. It is not a formality to rush through on your way to the closing table. Done well, it catches problems while you still have leverage to fix them. Done carelessly, it can leave you discovering issues after the home is legally yours. Here is how to do it right.

What the Final Walk-Through Is (and Is Not)

The single most important thing to understand is that the final walk-through is a confirmation, not a re-inspection. You are not there to renegotiate the deal or to evaluate the home as if you were seeing it for the first time. That work already happened earlier during your home inspection. The walk-through simply verifies that the home is in the same shape it was when you agreed to buy it, and that any promised repairs are done.

Think of it as answering three questions. Is the home in the condition I expected? Were the agreed-upon repairs completed? Did anything change since I last saw it? If the answer to all three is yes, you head to closing with confidence. If something is off, the walk-through is your chance to raise it before the sale is final, while you still have room to ask for a solution.

Because it is a confirmation rather than a fresh evaluation, the walk-through usually goes quickly when everything is in order. That is exactly the goal. But quick should never mean careless. The handful of minutes you spend testing systems and checking repairs is your final safeguard, and it is far easier to resolve an issue now than after the deed is in your name.

When the Walk-Through Happens

In most Central Indiana transactions, the final walk-through takes place shortly before closing, often the day before or the morning of. The timing is deliberate. You want it close enough to closing that the home's condition is unlikely to change afterward, but with enough breathing room that if you spot a problem, there is still time to address it before you sign.

Your agent will schedule and attend it with you. Give yourself enough time to be thorough rather than squeezing it into a few rushed minutes. Bring your paperwork, including the inspection report and any repair agreements, so you can check promised items against what you actually see. A little patience here protects one of the biggest purchases of your life.

It also helps to know who will not be there. In most cases the sellers have already moved out or are in the process, so the home may be empty, which actually makes it easier to spot damage and confirm what conveys. If the sellers are still occupying the home, note anything that could be affected by their final move-out and plan to confirm it is resolved before you close.

Your Central Indiana Final Walk-Through Checklist

Walk the home methodically, room by room, and do not assume anything works just because it looks fine. Turn things on. Open and close things. Here is a practical checklist to guide you.

  • Agreed repairs are done: confirm every repair from your negotiation was completed, and ask for receipts if professional work was required.
  • Systems work: test the heating and cooling, run faucets and showers, flush toilets, and check for hot water.
  • Appliances that convey: make sure the refrigerator, range, dishwasher, and any other included appliances are present and running.
  • Nothing was removed: confirm items meant to stay, like light fixtures, blinds, or a mounted television bracket, are still there.
  • No new damage: look for scrapes, holes, or damage from the sellers moving out.
  • Broom-clean condition: the home should be reasonably clean and empty of the sellers' belongings and trash.
  • Utilities are on: lights, outlets, and major systems should be powered so you can actually test them.
  • Windows and doors: open and lock them, and check that all keys, openers, and codes will be provided.

Pay special attention to areas that were hidden on your earlier visits. Look under sinks for leaks, check the spots where furniture used to sit for stains or damage, test the garage door opener, and peek at the water heater and furnace. Sellers moving out can reveal, or occasionally cause, issues that were not visible when the home was staged and full of belongings. A slow drip behind a washing machine or a gouge in the floor under a rug is exactly the kind of thing this visit exists to catch.

What to Do If Something Is Not Right

If your walk-through turns up a problem, do not panic and do not simply proceed hoping it works out. Tell your agent right away. Because you have not closed yet, you still have options, and the right one depends on what you find and what your contract says. Your remaining contingency protections and the specifics of your purchase agreement shape what you can ask for.

  • Ask the seller to fix it: for a promised repair that was not done, the seller can be asked to complete it before closing.
  • Negotiate a credit: the parties may agree to a closing credit so you can handle the issue yourself.
  • Delay the closing: if the problem is significant, closing can sometimes be postponed until it is resolved.
  • Use an escrow holdback: money can be set aside at closing to cover a repair that cannot be finished in time.

None of these should be handled on your own in the moment. This is exactly where an experienced agent earns their keep, negotiating calmly and protecting your position. The worst outcome is staying silent, closing anyway, and then owning a problem you could have solved a day earlier.

How the Walk-Through Leads Into Closing Day

When the walk-through checks out, you are cleared for the final step. Closing day is when ownership officially transfers, you sign your paperwork, and the keys become yours. A clean walk-through means you arrive at the table without last-minute surprises, which is exactly how it should feel. Treat the walk-through as your last checkpoint, not a lap of honor. Turn on every system, confirm every promised repair, and speak up before you sign, because your leverage disappears the moment you close.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is the final walk-through the same as a home inspection?

No. The inspection earlier in the process evaluates the home's condition in depth. The walk-through simply confirms the home is in the agreed condition and that promised repairs were completed.

What happens if I find a problem during the walk-through?

Tell your agent immediately, since you have not closed yet. Options can include asking the seller to fix it, negotiating a credit, delaying closing, or setting up an escrow holdback, depending on your contract.

When does the final walk-through happen in Indiana?

It usually takes place shortly before closing, often the day before or the morning of, so the home's condition is confirmed as close to closing as possible.
