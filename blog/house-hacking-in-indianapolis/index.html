House Hacking in Indianapolis: Live in One Unit, Rent the Rest

How to buy a duplex, triplex or fourplex (or rent out rooms), live in one unit and let rent cover part of the mortgage, with 2026 loan rules, Indianapolis requirements and a fully worked example.

Investment Properties Updated for 2026 Indianapolis

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Investment Properties November 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~10 min read

House Hacking in Indianapolis at a Glance

What is house hacking? Buying a home with two to four units (or a home with spare rooms), living in one unit and renting the others so tenants cover part of your mortgage. Because it’s your principal residence, you can use owner-occupant loans: FHA with 3.5% down, conventional with 5% down through Fannie Mae’s automated underwriting, or VA with no down payment for eligible veterans. Lenders generally count about 75% of the expected rent, and 3–4 unit FHA loans must pass a self-sufficiency test.

House hacking is one of the most practical ways to start investing in Indianapolis real estate: buy a duplex, triplex or fourplex, live in one unit and rent the others. Your tenants’ rent helps pay the mortgage while you build equity and learn landlording next door. It also comes with rules: occupancy requirements, lenders that discount the rent they’ll count, and Indianapolis’s own landlord registration. Below we cover 2026 loan options, how lenders count rent, a fully worked hypothetical example, local rules and the risks.

This is general information, not lending, legal or tax advice. Loan rules change and lenders can add their own requirements, so confirm the details with your lender, attorney or CPA.

What House Hacking Is (and Isn’t)

The classic version is a small multi-family property: you live in one unit and rent the other one to three. A lighter version is renting spare bedrooms in a single-family home. Either way, the property has to be your principal residence. That’s what unlocks owner-occupant financing with far smaller down payments than an investment-property loan, and it’s the commitment your lender holds you to. Our house hacking glossary entry covers the basics. The owner-occupant programs below stop at four units; buildings with five or more are generally financed with commercial loans.

Loan Options for 2–4 Unit Homes in 2026

FHA: 3.5% down

FHA loans are the most common house-hacking tool. Under HUD’s Single Family Housing Policy Handbook 4000.1 (updated August 12, 2026):

  • Down payment: 3.5% (a 96.5% maximum loan-to-value) with a minimum decision credit score of 580 or higher. Scores from 500 to 579 are limited to 90% loan-to-value, or 10% down.
  • Occupancy: at least one borrower must move in within 60 days of closing and intend to live there for at least one year. FHA generally won’t insure more than one principal residence per borrower at a time.
  • Self-sufficiency test (3–4 units only): take the appraiser’s fair market rent for all the units, including the one you’ll live in, and subtract the greater of the appraiser’s vacancy and maintenance estimate or 25%. What’s left has to cover the full monthly payment (principal, interest, taxes and insurance).
  • Reserves: 3–4 unit properties need three months of payments in verified reserves after closing.
  • Mortgage insurance: 1.75% upfront plus 0.55% a year for the life of the loan with 3.5% down (on base loans up to $726,200).

Conventional: 5% down through Fannie Mae

Fannie Mae’s November 18, 2023 update to Desktop Underwriter (DU), its automated underwriting system, raised the maximum loan-to-value on 2–4 unit principal residences, and its Eligibility Matrix now allows 95% loan-to-value, or 5% down, on those purchases. That applies to loans approved through DU, not manually underwritten or high-balance loans. DU also requires six months of reserves on a 2–4 unit principal residence, and if you use grant money above 80% loan-to-value, at least 5% must come from your own funds. Expect private mortgage insurance with less than 20% down. The occupancy rule mirrors FHA’s: move in within 60 days and stay at least a year.

VA: up to four units with no down payment

Eligible veterans and service members can use a VA-backed purchase loan on a home with up to four units, as long as they live in it, with no down payment if the price isn’t higher than the appraised value (VA). Counting rent is stricter: under VA’s underwriting rule (38 CFR 36.4340), the lender can only use rent from the other units if you can document that you’re likely to succeed as a landlord and you have cash reserves to cover the payments for at least six months without that rent. See our VA loan buyers page.

How Lenders Count the Rent

Lenders don’t credit every dollar of rent. The usual 25% haircut covers vacancy and upkeep:

  • FHA: with no rental history on the property, the lender uses 75% of the lesser of the appraiser’s fair market rent or the lease rent. That amount is added to your income; it doesn’t reduce the payment, which still counts in full in your debt-to-income ratio.
  • Fannie Mae: gross monthly rent times 75%, supported by the appraiser’s rent figures and any lease that transfers to you. With less than 12 months of property management experience, the rent can only offset the property’s own payment.
  • VA: generally 75% of the rent, and only with the landlord documentation and reserves described above.

Renting rooms is harder to qualify with. FHA only counts boarder income (rent from someone living inside your own unit) with a current 12-month history, capped at 30% of your qualifying income. For a first purchase, plan to qualify on your own income and treat roommate rent as a bonus.

Run the Numbers: A Hypothetical Duplex

Everything in this example is hypothetical and for illustration only. It isn’t a listing, a rent survey or a loan quote. Our assumptions:

  • Price: $250,000 for a duplex with two similar units.
  • Loan: FHA with 3.5% down ($8,750). The 1.75% upfront premium is financed, for a $245,472 loan, plus 0.55% a year in annual premium.
  • Rate: 7.28% on a 30-year fixed, Freddie Mac’s average for November 19, 2026.
  • Property taxes: 1.5% of the price a year, blending Indiana’s 1% homestead cap on your unit with the 2% cap on the rented unit. The caps are ceilings, so a real bill could be lower.
  • Insurance: $2,400 a year for a policy that covers the rented unit (get a real quote).
  • Rent on the other unit: $1,250 a month. Closing costs: $8,000.

Rent offset: the tenant covers about 54% of the payment. Set aside 15% of the rent ($187.50) for vacancy and repairs and your true cost is about $1,240 a month, while roughly $2,360 of your first year’s payments goes to principal. If you’d otherwise rent a unit like the one next door for $1,250, you’re living for about the same cost and building equity. What the lender sees: FHA would add 75% of the rent, or $937.50, to your monthly income and count the full $2,303 payment. With two units, there’s no self-sufficiency test.

The investor view: GRM, the 1% rule and cash-on-cash return

Now picture moving out after the first year and renting both units for $1,250 each, or $30,000 a year:

  • Gross rent multiplier (GRM): $250,000 ÷ $30,000 = 8.3. A lower GRM means more rent per dollar of price, but it ignores expenses.
  • The 1% rule: $2,500 of monthly rent is 1.0% of the price, so this duplex just passes a rough screen many investors use. Passing doesn’t make it a good deal.
  • Cash-on-cash return: annual cash flow divided by the cash you put in. Without the homestead cap, taxes rise to as much as $417 a month. Add $200 for insurance, $125 for 5% vacancy and $250 for repairs, and the property nets about $1,508 a month before the mortgage. Subtract the $1,790 loan payment and it loses about $282 a month, or $3,381 a year. On $16,750 of cash invested, that’s a cash-on-cash return of about −20%.

That’s the honest lesson at 7%-plus rates with 3.5% down: a property can pass the 1% rule and still lose money as a pure rental. Here, rents of about $1,450 per unit would turn cash flow slightly positive (about $58 a month, a 4% cash-on-cash return). The house hack works because you’re replacing your own rent. Try your own numbers with our mortgage calculator, and see how much house you can afford in Indianapolis.

Triplexes and fourplexes: the self-sufficiency hurdle

Now take a hypothetical $400,000 fourplex with an FHA loan and an appraised fair market rent of $1,000 per unit. HUD counts all four units, including yours: $4,000 minus 25% leaves $3,000. The payment (with taxes at a blended 1.75% and $3,600 a year of insurance) comes to about $3,748, or 125% of that figure, so it fails. Rents would need to be about $1,250 per unit to pass. Ask your lender to run this test before you write an offer.

Indianapolis Rules to Know Before You Buy

Register as a landlord

Owners or landlords of residential rental units in the City of Indianapolis have to register them with the city’s Department of Business and Neighborhood Services, a program that started January 1, 2015. You register online through the city’s Citizen Access portal, list an Indiana-based landlord or property manager contact and the number of rental units on each parcel, and renew every year; the city’s fact sheet lists a $5 fee covering all of one owner’s properties. Rentals in the excluded cities of Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway are exempt from the city registry, so check with those cities directly. Renting the other unit short-term? Since January 1, 2025, Indianapolis has required an annual permit for each short-term rental unit, with a one-time $150 fee on the first permit.

Indiana landlord–tenant basics

  • Security deposits: within 45 days after the lease ends and you get the unit back, you must return the deposit minus allowed deductions (such as unpaid rent, damage from the tenant not following the law or the lease, and unpaid utilities the tenant owed), with an itemized written notice. Your obligation starts once the tenant gives you a mailing address in writing. If you don’t comply, the tenant can recover the deposit plus reasonable attorney’s fees (IC 32-31-3-12).
  • Your duties: deliver the unit safe, clean and habitable, comply with health and housing codes, keep common areas reasonably clean, and keep the electrical, plumbing, sanitary, heating, ventilation and air conditioning systems in good working order (IC 32-31-8-5).

Property taxes on a house hack

Indiana caps property taxes at 1% of gross assessed value for a homestead and 2% for other residential property, a category that includes buildings with two or more dwelling units (Indiana DLGF). The 1% cap covers only your homestead, so expect the rented units to fall under the higher cap, and the whole building if you move out. Ask the assessor how a property is split, and see our guide to Indiana’s property tax caps.

Make sure every unit is legal

A basement or attic unit that was never permitted isn’t a legal unit, and it can cause problems with the appraisal, insurance and the city, so check zoning and permit history before you count on that rent. In our experience, 2–4 unit buildings are more common in Indianapolis’s older neighborhoods closer to downtown than in newer, mostly single-family suburbs, and their age and condition vary a lot. Our multi-family investing guide covers the market side.

The Risks: Vacancy, Repairs and Living Next to Your Tenant

  • Vacancy: in a duplex, one empty unit means no rent at all. Keep reserves beyond the minimums and budget for turnover.
  • Repairs: two furnaces, two water heaters, one roof. Older buildings can surprise you, so price in near-term replacements the inspection turns up.
  • Being a landlord to your neighbor: you’ll hear about the leaky faucet at 10 p.m. and have to enforce the lease with someone on the other side of the wall. Screen carefully and put everything in writing.
  • Insurance: standard homeowners policies are designed for personal risks, and many insurers treat regularly renting all or part of your home as business use (Triple-I). Tell your agent about the rented units and ask about landlord coverage, liability limits and lost rent. Tenants need their own renters policy.
  • Taxes: rent is taxable income. When you live in one unit of a duplex, the IRS has you split shared expenses such as mortgage interest and property taxes between the rental and personal parts, and you can depreciate the rental part (IRS Publication 527). Ask a CPA.
  • Occupancy is a commitment: misstating your plans on a loan application can have serious legal consequences. If your plans change, talk to your lender.

Next Steps

  1. Get pre-approved with a lender who does 2–4 unit loans. Not every loan officer regularly handles multi-unit FHA, conventional or VA loans. Ask how they’ll count rent, what reserves you need and, for 3–4 units, whether a property passes self-sufficiency. See our pre-approval guide.
  2. Run every property both ways: your net cost while you live there, and the full-rental cash flow if you move out.
  3. Work with an agent who knows small multi-family. We can pull rent comparables, flag units that may not be legal and write an offer with the right inspection and appraisal protections. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I buy a duplex with an FHA loan in Indianapolis?

Yes. FHA insures homes with up to four units as long as at least one borrower moves in within 60 days of closing and intends to live there at least a year. With a credit score of 580 or higher, the minimum down payment is 3.5%. In Marion County, the 2026 FHA loan limit is $693,050 for a two-unit property.

How much do I need to put down on a fourplex I'll live in?

As little as 3.5% with FHA (580+ credit score), 5% with a conventional loan approved through Fannie Mae's Desktop Underwriter, or nothing with a VA loan if you're eligible and the price doesn't exceed the appraised value. FHA also requires three months of payments in reserves on a 3-4 unit property, and Fannie Mae's DU requires six months on a 2-4 unit principal residence.

What is the FHA self-sufficiency test?

It applies to FHA loans on three- and four-unit properties. The appraiser's fair market rent for all units, including the one you'll live in, minus the greater of the appraiser's vacancy and maintenance estimate or 25%, must be at least equal to the monthly principal, interest, taxes and insurance.

Will the lender count the rent from the other units?

Usually, but not all of it. FHA, Fannie Mae and VA generally count about 75% of the expected rent, based on the appraiser's rent estimate or a lease. FHA adds it to your income rather than subtracting it from the payment, Fannie Mae limits how it's used if you have less than 12 months of property management experience, and VA requires landlord documentation and six months of reserves.

Do I have to register a rental with the City of Indianapolis?

Yes, if the rental unit is inside the City of Indianapolis. Owners or landlords of residential rental units must register with the Department of Business and Neighborhood Services and renew every year. Properties in the excluded cities of Beech Grove, Lawrence, Southport and Speedway are exempt from the city registry. Short-term rentals need a separate annual permit.

How long do I have to live in a house hack?

FHA and Fannie Mae both expect you to move in within 60 days of closing and live there for at least one year. After that you can generally move out and keep the property as a rental, but talk to your lender about your plans, and expect the property taxes on your unit to rise once it's no longer your homestead.
