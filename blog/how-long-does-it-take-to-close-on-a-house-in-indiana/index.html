How Long Does It Take to Close on a House in Indiana?

From accepted offer to keys: a step-by-step Indiana closing timeline, how cash and FHA or VA loans change it, and what tends to push a closing date back.

Buying a Home Closing Timeline Indiana

A cash purchase can often close in a couple of weeks, once the title work and any inspection period are done.

Once your offer is accepted, the next question is always the same: when do we get the keys? In Central Indiana, most financed purchases close about 30 to 45 days after the offer is accepted, and cash deals can move much faster. Here’s what happens in between, roughly week by week, and what tends to push a closing date back. If you’re earlier in the process, start with our guide to the home buying process.

The Short Answer: About 30 to 45 Days With a Mortgage

Your purchase agreement sets the closing date, and most financed purchases in Central Indiana are written to close about 30 to 45 days after acceptance. National data lines up with that. According to ICE’s May 2026 Mortgage Monitor, the average purchase loan closed in 36.8 days in March 2026, down from 37.3 days a year earlier and the fastest monthly average since ICE began tracking it in 2019. The typical loan took 11 days to go from application to rate lock and another 26 days from lock to closing.

One caveat: ICE counts from the loan application, not the accepted offer. Most buyers make a formal application right after going under contract, so the two clocks are close but not identical. Chase, for example, puts the average at about 43 days from accepted offer to keys (January 2026).

A Typical Indiana Closing Timeline

Every contract sets its own deadlines, so treat these as typical ranges for a financed purchase, not rules:

Offer acceptedDay 0The purchase agreement is signed and the closing date is set. Your agent sends the contract to your lender and the title company.
Earnest moneyWithin 2 banking daysIndiana rules require the listing broker to deposit it in escrow, or with whoever the contract names, within two banking days of final acceptance.
Loan applicationDays 1–3You complete the formal application. Your lender must send a Loan Estimate within three business days.
Home inspectionWeek 1–2The inspection usually happens in the first week or two, followed by any repair requests within your contract’s deadlines.
AppraisalWeeks 1–3Your lender orders the appraisal to confirm the home supports the price.
Title search and surveyWeeks 1–3The title company checks ownership and liens and issues a title commitment. A survey is ordered if your lender requires one or you want one.

A few of those steps deserve more detail. Your contract’s contingencies come with firm deadlines, and the home inspection is usually the first one. The appraisal runs in parallel, and if it comes in below the price, our guide to a low appraisal in Indiana walks through your options. On the title side, the title company searches the public record and issues a title commitment, and some buyers or lenders add a survey (here are the survey types).

In Indiana, the closing itself usually takes place at a title company. The state doesn’t require an attorney at a residential closing, although you can hire one if your situation is complicated. For what happens at the table, see our final walk-through checklist and what to expect on closing day.

Closing With Cash

Paying cash removes the slowest parts of the process: there’s no loan application, no lender-required appraisal and no underwriting. That’s why cash buyers can often close in about a couple of weeks, as Chase notes. The limits are the title search, any inspection period you negotiated, and the seller’s own moving timeline. Even with cash, we recommend an inspection and an owner’s title insurance policy, and you’ll need proof of funds and good funds for closing, usually a verified wire.

FHA, VA and Conventional Loans

  • Conventional loans. Starting November 2, 2026, appraisals on most conventional loans use Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac’s redesigned report, which asks for more detail. Appraisals may take a little longer while appraisers adjust. Our UAD 3.6 explainer covers what changes.
  • FHA loans. The appraiser also checks the home against HUD’s minimum property requirements. If repairs are required, they may need to be finished, and sometimes re-inspected, before the loan can close, which can add days or weeks. See our guide to FHA loans in Indiana.
  • VA loans. The VA assigns the appraiser. Its timeliness standard in Indiana is 8 business days, or 7 in Marion, Hamilton, Allen and Lake counties (VA table effective May 1, 2026), counted from the first business day after the assignment. More in our VA loan guide.

What Delays a Closing

  • A low appraisal. If the value comes in under the price, you, the seller or both need to agree on a fix, and that negotiation takes time.
  • Repair negotiations. Back-and-forth over inspection items, or waiting on contractor quotes, can stretch past the original deadlines.
  • Title problems. An unreleased old mortgage, a lien, an estate that hasn’t been settled or a boundary question has to be cleared before the title company can insure the sale.
  • Paperwork and credit changes. Missing documents slow underwriting, and a new car loan, credit card or job change before closing can force the lender to re-qualify you.
  • Changes to the loan. If the APR becomes inaccurate, the loan product changes or a prepayment penalty is added after you get your Closing Disclosure, federal rules require a corrected one and a new three-business-day wait. For this rule, Saturdays count as business days, but Sundays and federal holidays don’t.
  • Holidays. Banks and many lenders and title companies close on federal holidays, including Thanksgiving (November 26, 2026), Christmas (December 25), New Year’s Day (January 1, 2027) and Martin Luther King Jr. Day (January 18, 2027). A late-December closing needs extra cushion.

How to Keep Your Closing on Schedule

  • Get fully pre-approved before you offer, not just pre-qualified. Here’s the difference.
  • Answer your lender fast. Send requested documents the same day if you can, and don’t open new credit or change jobs until after closing.
  • Book the inspection right away, so you have time to negotiate inside your deadline.
  • Line up homeowners insurance early. Your lender needs proof of coverage before it will clear you to close.
  • Read your Closing Disclosure the day it arrives and ask about anything that doesn’t match your Loan Estimate.
  • Protect your wire. Call your title company at a number you already know to confirm wiring instructions, and never trust changes that arrive by email. Our buyer closing checklist and wire fraud explainer cover the details.

Our Take

For most Central Indiana buyers with a mortgage, a 30- to 45-day closing is realistic, and a well-organized deal can land at the shorter end. Cash can be faster, but the title search and your inspection period still set the pace. The closings that slip are usually the ones where a deadline gets missed or a document sits in an inbox. Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to close on a house in Indiana?

Most financed purchases in Indiana close about 30 to 45 days after the offer is accepted. Nationally, ICE reported that the average purchase loan closed 36.8 days after application in March 2026. Cash purchases can often close in a couple of weeks.

How fast can you close on a house with cash?

Often in about two weeks, sometimes faster. Without a lender there is no required appraisal or underwriting, so the main limits are the title search, any inspection period in your contract and the seller's moving plans.

Do you need a lawyer to close on a house in Indiana?

No. Indiana does not require an attorney at a residential closing. Title companies typically handle the closing, including the title search, documents, funds and recording. You can still hire a real estate attorney if your situation is complicated.

When will I get my Closing Disclosure?

Federal rules require you to receive it at least three business days before closing. For this rule Saturdays count as business days, but Sundays and federal holidays do not. If the APR becomes inaccurate, the loan product changes or a prepayment penalty is added, you get a corrected disclosure and a new three-business-day wait.

What can delay a closing in Indiana?

The most common causes are a low appraisal, drawn-out repair negotiations, title problems such as an unreleased lien or an unsettled estate, missing documents or new debt during underwriting, loan changes that restart the Closing Disclosure waiting period, and holidays when banks and many lenders are closed.

Can you close on a house in two weeks with a mortgage?

It is possible but tight. The lender has to order the appraisal, verify your income and assets and get you the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. In March 2026 the average purchase loan took 11 days from application to rate lock and another 26 days to close, according to ICE, so most buyers should plan on 30 days or more.
