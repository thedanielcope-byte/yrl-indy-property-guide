How to File Your Indiana Homestead Deduction: County by County

Just bought a home? Filing is free, you only do it once, and it’s the biggest property tax break most Indiana homeowners get. Here’s exactly where to file in 17 counties.

Property Taxes Deadline: January 15 17 Counties

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Filing the Indiana Homestead Deduction at a Glance

How do I file for the homestead deduction in Indiana? File State Form 5473 (Form HC10), or your county’s online version, with the auditor of the county where the home is. It must be filed or postmarked by January 15 of the year the taxes are due, and you file once, not every year.

The homestead deduction is the single biggest property tax break most Indiana homeowners get, and it isn’t automatic. You have to file for it, once, with the county auditor. Plenty of buyers assume the title company handled it at closing and find out a year later, when the first full tax bill arrives, that it didn’t happen.

This guide covers the filing itself: the deadline, what you’ll need, and where to file in each of the 17 counties we serve, with links to each county’s online form or paper process. For what the deduction is worth and how it’s changing through 2031, see our Indiana homestead exemption explainer.

This is general information, not tax or legal advice. County procedures change; confirm details with your county auditor or the Indiana Department of Local Government Finance (in.gov/dlgf).

Before You File: Who Qualifies

Indiana law calls it the homestead standard deduction (most people say “homestead exemption”). You qualify if the home is your principal place of residence in Indiana and you:

  • Own it, or are buying it under a contract recorded with the county recorder that makes you responsible for the property taxes.
  • Actually live there when you sign the form. The state form says you must be using the property as your principal residence at the time of signing, so file after you move in, not before.
  • Don’t claim another homestead. Only one individual or married couple can receive the deduction on a home, and a person or couple can’t claim two homes in the same year.

The deduction covers the house, one garage and up to one acre around it. Any part of the home that produces income, such as the other side of a duplex you rent out or a shop run out of the garage, doesn’t qualify. Homes owned through a trust or an LLC can qualify in some cases; the county will ask for documents.

The Deadline: January 15

The current state form (State Form 5473, revised July 2025) says the application must be completed, dated and filed or postmarked with the county auditor on or before January 15 of the year the property taxes are first due. Once approved, it applies that year and every year after while you still qualify.

In practice: if you close and move in during 2026, file by January 15, 2027, and the deduction applies to the property taxes you pay in 2027. Miss it, and you pay that year’s taxes without the deduction; file anyway and it starts the following year.

Don’t cut it close. Several county websites still show December 31 or January 5, dates from older versions of the rule, and one shows May 10. The safest move is to file the week you move in. If you’re closing in late December, aim to file before the new year.

What You’ll Need

Online or on paper, the form asks for the same things:

  • Legal names of you and your spouse, if married.
  • The last five digits of each person’s Social Security number. If you don’t have one, the last five digits of a driver’s license or state ID and the issuing state. State law keeps these numbers confidential.
  • Your parcel number, which is on your closing documents and the county’s property search. Most online forms let you look it up by address.
  • Any homestead you’re leaving. The form asks whether you own property elsewhere and the address of a homestead you’re vacating.
  • An email address for online filing, so you get a confirmation. Save it.

Did Your Title Company Already File It?

In many Indiana closings, the title company includes the homestead application in your closing paperwork and sends it to the auditor. Johnson County even says your closing company normally files it for you. But it doesn’t always happen, and Hancock County specifically asks owners to confirm that forms filed by title companies, lenders or agents went in on time.

So check. Most counties below have an online lookup that shows whether your parcel has the deduction filed. If it isn’t there a few weeks after closing, file it yourself. Filing a duplicate is far better than missing a year. It’s on our buyer closing checklist for exactly this reason.

How to File in Each County

Every county below was checked against its official website in November 2026. Jump to yours:

Marion · Hamilton · Hendricks · Johnson · Hancock · Boone · Madison · Morgan · Shelby · Bartholomew · Brown · Decatur · Jackson · Jennings · Montgomery · Parke · Putnam

Marion County

  • File online: Homestead Deduction Applicationon indy.gov. Only the owner or an authorized representative can apply. Keep the confirmation email: the county says it’s your only receipt.
  • Where: Marion County Auditor’s Office, City-County Building, 200 E. Washington St., Suite W-125, Indianapolis, IN 46204
  • Phone: 317-327-4646
  • Already filed? Check Search Current Deductions or Credits on Your Property “Pending” or “In-Process” means you’ve done your part.
  • Good to know: Marion asks you to re-file when the title changes, such as adding or removing an owner or moving the home into a trust.

Official county page: Marion County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Marion County real estate guide.

Hamilton County

  • File online: Property Tax Deduction Forms. Look up your address or parcel number, choose the homestead claim and the county emails you a PDF copy.
  • Where: Hamilton County Auditor, Real Property Department, Historic Courthouse, 33 N. 9th St., 1st floor (southwest corner), Noblesville, IN 46060
  • Phone: 317-770-4412
  • Already filed? Check Hamilton County Property Reports (the Current Deductions tab).
  • Good to know: Hamilton’s own pages show different deadlines (December 31, January 5 and January 15), so don’t wait. The county also says letters from Tax Management Associates about homestead eligibility are legitimate and should be answered.

Official county page: Hamilton County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Hamilton County real estate guide.

Hendricks County

  • File online: LowTaxInfo. Search your property, open Property Details and click File E-Forms. Homestead is the only deduction Hendricks takes online; the rest are filed in person.
  • Where: Hendricks County Auditor’s Office, 355 S. Washington St., #220, Danville, IN 46122 (8 a.m.–4 p.m., no appointment needed)
  • Phone: 317-745-9300
  • Already filed? Check LowTaxInfo: each parcel’s Property Details shows “Homestead Credit Filed?”
  • Good to know: Hendricks lists December 31 on some pages and January 15 on others. File as soon as you’ve moved in.

Official county page: Hendricks County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Hendricks County real estate guide.

Johnson County

  • File online: Johnson County’s Beacon property search. Find your address, open the property summary and use the “Apply online for Indiana Homestead Property Tax Deduction” link at the very bottom. You sign with a mouse, so use a computer.
  • Where: Johnson County Auditor’s Office, Government West Annex, 86 W. Court St., Franklin, IN 46131 (mail to the same address)
  • Phone: 317-346-4310
  • Already filed? Check Beacon, which the county says shows deductions for each parcel.
  • Good to know: On a new purchase, the county says your closing company normally files the homestead for you, so confirm it did before filing again. Refinancing doesn’t require re-filing.

Official county page: Johnson County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Johnson County real estate guide.

Hancock County

  • File online: Applying Online for Homestead Deductions, the county’s step-by-step page. It takes you to Beacon: search your parcel and use “Apply online for Homestead Property Tax Deduction” at the bottom of the property summary.
  • Where: Hancock County Auditor’s Office, Courthouse Annex, 111 S. American Legion Place, Suite 217, Greenfield, IN 46140
  • Phone: 317-477-1105
  • Already filed? Check LowTaxInfo: each parcel’s Property Details shows “Homestead Credit Filed?”
  • Good to know: If a title company, lender or real estate agent filed your form, Hancock asks you to call and confirm it was filed on time. Refinancing doesn’t affect deductions.

Official county page: Hancock County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Hancock County real estate guide.

Boone County

  • How to file: Paper form only: Form HC10, filed in person or by mail. Boone posts it on the Auditor’s forms page; the current version is on the state forms site.
  • Where: Boone County Auditor’s Office, Boone County Courthouse (2nd floor, southeast corner), 201 Courthouse Square, Lebanon, IN 46052
  • Phone: 765-482-2940
  • Already filed? Call the Auditor’s office, or check the deductions listed on your latest tax bill.
  • Good to know: Boone’s pages still give a January 5 postmark date from an older version of the rule. File well before either date.

Official county page: Boone County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Boone County real estate guide.

Madison County

  • How to file: Download the Homestead Standard/Supplemental form from the Auditor’s Resources page, then mail it or bring it in. The county mentions online filing, but its deduction links weren’t working when we checked in November 2026.
  • Where: Madison County Auditor’s Office, Government Center, 16 E. 9th St., Room 101, Anderson, IN 46016
  • Phone: 765-641-9419 (deductions staff: 765-641-9422 or 765-641-9505)
  • Already filed? Check Treasurer’s property tax search (the Deductions/Exemptions section of each parcel).
  • Good to know: The county asks for your Indiana driver’s license and the last five digits of your Social Security number, plus the same for your spouse, and stresses adding your email address to the form.

Official county page: Madison County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Madison County real estate guide.

Morgan County

  • How to file: Paper form (Form HC10 from the state forms site), filed in person, by mail, by fax (765-342-1015) or by email to auditor@morgancounty.in.gov.
  • Where: Morgan County Auditor, 180 S. Main St., Suite 104, Martinsville, IN 46151
  • Phone: 765-342-1001
  • Already filed? Check LowTaxInfo: each parcel’s Property Details shows “Homestead Credit Filed?”
  • Good to know: Mailing it? The county asks you to include a phone number and a self-addressed, stamped envelope for your receipt.

Official county page: Morgan County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Morgan County real estate guide.

Shelby County

  • How to file: No online filing. Shelby’s forms page doesn’t include the homestead form, so use Form HC10 from the state forms site and file it with the Auditor.
  • Where: Shelby County Auditor, County Annex, 25 W. Polk St., Room 104, Shelbyville, IN 46176 (the Annex is open Monday–Thursday)
  • Phone: 317-392-6310
  • Already filed? Check Auditor’s property tax search (the Deductions/Exemptions section of each parcel).
  • Good to know: Call ahead if you plan to mail or email the form; the county site doesn’t say.

Official county page: Shelby County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Shelby County real estate guide.

Bartholomew County

  • How to file: Paper form. The Auditor’s page posts the current homestead claim form; questions go to deductions@bartholomew.in.gov.
  • Where: Bartholomew County Auditor, Deductions Department (1st floor), 440 3rd St., Columbus, IN 47201
  • Phone: 812-379-1510
  • Already filed? Ask the Deductions Department, or check the deductions listed on your latest tax bill.
  • Good to know: Any time a parcel’s ownership changes, the county asks you to check with the Deductions Department so your deductions continue.

Official county page: Bartholomew County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Bartholomew County real estate guide.

Brown County

  • How to file: Paper form, filed in person or by mail. Brown’s posted form is an older version, so use the current Form HC10 from the state forms site.
  • Where: Brown County Auditor, 201 Locust Lane, 1st floor, Nashville, IN 47448 (mail: P.O. Box 37, Nashville, IN 47448)
  • Phone: 812-988-5485
  • Already filed? Call the Auditor, or check the deductions listed on your latest tax bill.
  • Good to know: Deductions go through the Auditor, not the Assessor. Brown’s Assessor FAQ still lists an older December 31 date.

Official county page: Brown County Auditor · Buying or selling here? See our Brown County real estate guide.

  • How to file: No online filing, and the county doesn’t post the form. Take Form HC10 from the state forms site to the Auditor’s office.
  • Where: Decatur County Auditor, Decatur County Courthouse, 150 Courthouse Square, Suite 133, Greensburg, IN 47240

  • Where: Jackson County Auditor’s Office, 111 S. Main St., Brownstown, IN 47220

  • Where: Jennings County Auditor, 200 E. Brown St., Vernon, IN 47282 (mail: P.O. Box 383, Vernon, IN 47282)

  • Where: Montgomery County Auditor, Government Center, 1580 Constitution Row, Suite A, Crawfordsville, IN 47933 (the office moved out of the courthouse)

When You Move, Rent It Out or Change the Deed

  • You don’t re-file every year. The Indiana DLGF says deductions carry over; you reapply only when the property is sold or the title changes.
  • Re-file when the deed changes. Adding or removing an owner, a marriage or divorce that changes the title, or moving the home into a trust can all require a new application. Refinancing alone doesn’t.
  • Tell the auditor within 60 days if you stop qualifying. If you move out or turn the home into a rental, file a certified statement with the county auditor (the state’s Homestead Deduction Change of Use Form). Miss the 60 days and you owe back the deduction plus a 10% civil penalty.
  • Moving mid-year? If you move from one Indiana homestead to another after the January 1 assessment date, the old home keeps its deduction for that tax cycle, and you can apply on the new one for the same cycle. The old one comes off the next year.

Other Deductions You File Separately

The supplemental homestead deduction comes with the standard deduction: the state form says the county auditor applies it for you. Other benefits need their own application with the same auditor, including the over-65 deduction and credit, the blind or disabled deduction and the disabled veteran deductions. See Indiana property tax deductions for seniors for those, and Indiana property tax caps explained for how the 1% homestead cap works.

Frequently Asked Questions

When is the deadline to file for the homestead deduction in Indiana?

The current state form says the application must be completed, dated and filed or postmarked with the county auditor on or before January 15 of the year the property taxes are first due. If you close and move in during 2026, file by January 15, 2027 for the deduction to apply to taxes paid in 2027. Some county websites still list older dates, so file as soon as you move in.

Do I have to file for the homestead deduction every year?

No. Once it's approved, the deduction carries over each year while you own and live in the home. You reapply only if the property is sold or the title changes, such as adding or removing an owner or moving the home into a trust. Refinancing alone doesn't require a new application.

Can I file for the homestead deduction online in Indiana?

In most of the Indianapolis area, yes. Marion, Hamilton, Hendricks, Johnson and Hancock counties all have online filing, as does Putnam County. Boone, Morgan and several smaller counties use the paper state form (Form HC10), filed in person, by mail and in some counties by email.

How do I know if my homestead deduction was filed at closing?

Check your county's property or tax lookup, which in most counties shows whether a parcel has the homestead deduction. Your property tax bill also lists the deductions applied. If it isn't there a few weeks after closing, call the county auditor or file it yourself.

What happens if I miss the January 15 deadline?

You pay that year's property taxes without the deduction. File anyway: the application then applies to the following year's taxes and every year after while you qualify.

Do I need a separate form for the supplemental homestead deduction?

No. The state form says the county auditor applies the supplemental deduction for anyone who qualifies for the standard homestead deduction. Over-65, disability and veteran deductions do need their own applications.
