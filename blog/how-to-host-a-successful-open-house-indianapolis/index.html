How to Host a Successful Open House in Indianapolis

A seller's playbook: prep the home, time it right, promote it, and add a virtual option to reach every buyer.

Selling a Home Seller Guide Central Indiana

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Selling a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

A great open house does not happen by accident. The homes that draw a crowd — and real interest — are prepped, timed, and promoted with intention, and increasingly they are paired with a virtual option so distant buyers can attend too. Here is a practical playbook for Central Indiana sellers, whether you are working with an agent or just want to understand what good looks like. And here is how Your Realty Link folds open houses into a complete plan to sell your home.

Prep the Home to Show Its Best

An open house is a showing on a bigger stage, so the fundamentals of presentation matter even more. Declutter and depersonalize so buyers can picture themselves, not you. Deep-clean everything, especially kitchens and bathrooms. Let in as much light as possible — open blinds, turn on lamps, and swap dim bulbs. Handle small repairs that catch the eye: a sticky door, a running toilet, a scuffed wall. If your home is vacant or sparsely furnished, consider light staging so rooms feel warm and give a sense of scale.

Do not forget curb appeal — the first impression starts at the street. Mow, trim, sweep the walk, and add a clean doormat and a few plants near the entry.

Time It for Maximum Traffic

Timing matters more than most sellers realize. The strongest open houses usually land during launch week, when your listing is fresh and buyer attention is highest. Weekend midday windows tend to draw the most visitors in Central Indiana. Avoid holiday weekends and try not to compete head-to-head with major local events that will pull your audience elsewhere.

Promote It So People Actually Come

An unpromoted open house is just an empty house with the lights on. Make sure it is posted to the MIBOR MLS with the correct date and time so it flows to the major search portals. Add clear directional signage the day of. Share it on social media and in neighborhood groups, and let neighbors know — they often know someone looking to move nearby. A little promotion is the difference between three visitors and thirty.

Add a Virtual Option

The best modern open houses are not in-person only. Offering a virtual, live-video option lets serious buyers who can not attend still experience your home — a real advantage for reaching the out-of-town and relocation buyers moving to Central Indiana for work. Even one motivated remote buyer can be the one who writes the offer. At Your Realty Link, we can pair your open house with private live-video showings so no serious buyer is left out.

Keep It Safe and Sensible

Before you open the doors, put away valuables, jewelry, medications, spare keys, and sensitive documents. Never leave a home open and unattended — it should always be hosted by an agent. These small steps let you enjoy the upside of an open house without the risks. Not sure your price is right before you invite the public in? Start with a free home valuation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What day and time is best for an open house in Indianapolis?

Weekend midday windows generally draw the most traffic, and the most valuable open houses tend to happen during launch week, when your listing is newest. Avoid holiday weekends and big competing local events.

Should I be home during my own open house?

Usually no. Buyers are more comfortable speaking freely and lingering when the owner is not present, and your agent can host and field questions objectively. Plan to be out for the window.

Do open houses work for higher-priced or rural homes?

They can, but the strategy shifts. For luxury and rural properties, a targeted approach — including virtual live-video showings for distant, qualified buyers — often beats relying on foot traffic alone. Your Realty Link tailors the plan to your home and market.
