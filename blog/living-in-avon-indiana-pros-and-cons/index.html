Living in Avon, Indiana: Pros and Cons

An honest look at Avon: home prices, taxes, schools, the commute, neighborhoods and who it suits best, from a local Hendricks County team.

Living in Avon at a Glance

Is Avon, Indiana a good place to live? Avon is a good fit for west-side buyers who want newer homes and value. Prices typically run from the $250s to the $450s, Avon Community School Corporation serves the town, and IU Health West Hospital sits on Ronald Reagan Parkway. Expect US-36 congestion, a higher county income tax than Hamilton County, and a town center that’s still taking shape.

Avon is the west side’s family suburb: newer neighborhoods, Avon Community School Corporation and homes that typically run from the $250s to the $450s. What it doesn’t have yet is a traditional downtown, and US-36 carries most of the traffic.

Pros of Living in Avon

  • Homes typically run from the $250s to the $450s, strong value for the west side.
  • Avon Community School Corporation serves the town, with family neighborhoods along US-36 and County Road 100 N.
  • IU Health West Hospital is right in town on Ronald Reagan Parkway.
  • The population grew about 17% from 2020 to 2025, so newer neighborhoods offer plenty of choice.

Cons of Living in Avon

  • US-36 (Rockville Road) is the main road through Avon, and INDOT’s widening plan won’t start its first phase until 2030.
  • Hendricks County’s 1.70% local income tax is higher than Hamilton County’s 1.10%, though below Marion County’s 2.02%.
  • Avon has no traditional downtown yet; the Easton Grey project with its Riverwalk District broke ground in 2024 and is still being built.

Cost of Living in Avon

Home prices. Homes in Avon typically range from the $250s to the $450s. MIBOR reports the market by county: in October 2026, Hendricks County’s median sale price was $378,950, up 6.1% from a year earlier, homes took a median 33 days to sell (versus 20 a year earlier) and there were 3.3 months of supply. Prices in Avon itself can run higher or lower than the county figure. See our monthly market updates for the latest numbers.

Taxes. Hendricks County’s 1.70% local income tax (2026) is below Marion County’s 2.02%, about $320 a year less per $100,000 of income, though Hamilton County’s 1.10% is lower still. On property taxes, Indiana caps the bill on your primary home at 1% of its gross assessed value (not counting voter-approved referendums), and the homestead deduction lowers the taxable value, but only if it’s filed. Our homestead exemption guide explains how it works, and property taxes by county compares the metro.

Neighborhoods and Communities in Avon

Popular areas include family subdivisions along US-36, the Avon Town Hall Park area, newer communities near Ronald Reagan Parkway and established neighborhoods off County Road 100 N.

We profile 13 Avon communities, including Parks at Prestwick, Beechwood Farms, Stratford of Avon, Bridgewater, Winton Meadows, Austin Lakes, Avon Estates and Westover Woods. Browse them all in our communities directory.

Schools in Avon

Avon is served by Avon Community School Corporation.

Public school districts: Avon Community School Corporation.

Private schools in Avon: Our Shepherd Lutheran School.

School assignment follows the address, so confirm boundaries with the district before you write an offer. Our Central Indiana schools guide covers every district.

Commute and Getting Around

Avon is about 25 minutes to downtown Indianapolis via US-36 (Rockville Road) and I-74. Rush-hour times run longer, so test-drive the commute at the hours you’d actually drive it. Comparing drives? See the best Indianapolis suburbs for commuters.

  • Avon Night at the Park (each summer): Avon hosts Night at the Park each summer at Avon Town Hall with food trucks, live music, and a fireworks display.

Who Avon Suits

Avon suits west-side families who want newer homes, solid schools and value within about 25 minutes of downtown. Buyers who want a historic, walkable square should look at Danville.
