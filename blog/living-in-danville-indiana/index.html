Living in Danville, Indiana: Historic Charm Under the Courthouse Dome

Hendricks County’s historic seat pairs a landmark courthouse square and strong schools with easy US-36 access to Indianapolis about 30 minutes east.

City Guides August 2026 By Daniel Cope
City Guides August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~6 min read

For buyers weighing living in Danville, Indiana, the appeal usually clicks the moment they see the square. Danville is the seat of Hendricks County, sitting along US-36 about 30 minutes west of downtown Indianapolis, and its downtown is built around one of the most striking courthouses in the state. It is a genuine small city — historic architecture, strong schools, and a real commercial base minutes away in Avon and Brownsburg — often at friendlier prices than those larger neighbors. Here is what our team wants you to know before you make the move.

The Town’s Character

Danville has one of the strongest small-city downtowns in the Indianapolis metro, and it is impossible to miss the reason: the Hendricks County Courthouse, a Romanesque limestone landmark completed in 1914, towers over a square ringed with restored brick storefronts. Independent restaurants like the long-running Mayberry Cafe and Frank’s, plus shops, breweries, and the historic Royal Theater for live entertainment, keep the square busy day and night.

Beyond downtown, Ellis Park offers a pool, sports, and event space, and the Vandalia Trail along the historic National Road adds walking and biking miles. The Hendricks County 4-H Fair, the Mayberry Festival, and the fall Heartland Apple Festival at nearby Beasley’s Orchard keep the calendar full. To see how the housing fits around all this, browse our Danville real estate guide.

Housing Market & Price Ranges

The Danville market offers an unusually wide range of housing styles and price points — a natural result of being the county seat. Homes typically range from the $250s for established single-family homes and historic properties near downtown to the $400s and beyond for newer construction and larger homes in subdivisions on the city’s edges.

The historic neighborhoods around the courthouse square are a real draw: restored Victorians, Craftsman bungalows, and early-20th-century homes on tree-lined streets. Newer development along US-36 and the SR-39 corridor brings modern floor plans and amenities. Because Danville often prices below Avon and Brownsburg, it keeps attracting buyers who want Hendricks County living for less. Our Hendricks County real estate overview shows how the towns compare.

Schools in Danville

Danville is served by the Danville Community School Corporation, which operates Danville Community High School, Danville Community Middle School, and feeder elementary schools across the district. The system is well regarded for its academic, career-technical, and athletic programs, and the Warriors draw enthusiastic community support.

Families moving to Hendricks County who want a strong public district at attainable prices frequently land on Danville after weighing their options. As always, the attendance area reaches beyond the city limits, so our team confirms exactly which schools serve a home before you write an offer — a small step that prevents big surprises.

Commuting & Getting Around

Danville commuters benefit from direct US-36 access, with downtown Indianapolis about 20 miles east — roughly a 30-minute drive in normal conditions. The airport corridor is around 25 minutes via SR-267 south to I-70, and I-465 ties into the north-side employment centers in Carmel and Fishers.

Just as important, the Avon and Brownsburg retail and employment corridors sit only 10 to 15 minutes east, so Hendricks County’s major shopping and services are close without living on top of them. For buyers who want small-city character with full metro access, Danville delivers strong commute math at lower price points than its bigger neighbors.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is it like living in Danville, Indiana?

Living in Danville means a landmark courthouse square, historic architecture, strong schools, and a full community calendar, all about 30 minutes west of downtown Indianapolis via US-36.

How much do homes cost in Danville, Indiana?

Homes in Danville typically range from the $250s for established and historic homes near downtown to the $400s and beyond for newer construction and larger homes in newer subdivisions.

What schools serve Danville?

Danville is served by the Danville Community School Corporation, including Danville Community High School, Danville Community Middle School, and feeder elementary schools known for strong academics and athletics.

How far is Danville from Indianapolis?

US-36 runs straight east into the metro, so downtown Indianapolis is about 20 miles and roughly 30 minutes away, with Avon and Brownsburg only 10 to 15 minutes east.
