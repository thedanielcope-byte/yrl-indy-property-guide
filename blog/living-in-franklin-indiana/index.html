Living in Franklin, Indiana: A College Town with a Real Courthouse Square

Johnson County’s county seat pairs a genuine courthouse square and a college-town calendar with affordable homes about 30 minutes from downtown Indy.

City Guides August 2026 By Daniel Cope
City Guides August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~6 min read

If you have been quietly researching living in Franklin, Indiana, you have probably noticed something the glossy suburb brochures miss: this is a real town, not a subdivision with a name. Franklin is the county seat of Johnson County, roughly 25 to 30 minutes south of downtown Indianapolis, and it has grown without trading away its courthouse-square character. Home to Franklin College and one of Indiana’s most beloved county fairs, it offers space, affordability, and a genuine sense of place. Here is what our team at Your Realty Link tells buyers who are weighing a move to Franklin.

The Town’s Character

Franklin’s greatest asset is that it is authentic. The downtown Franklin real estate market is anchored by a genuine courthouse square — a walkable core of locally owned shops, coffee spots, and restaurants that has served Johnson County for generations, rather than a manufactured town center built to look old. Franklin College, a respected liberal arts school founded in 1834, adds a steady current of lectures, athletics, and cultural programming you rarely find in a town this size.

The rhythm of the year here is local. The Johnson County Fair fills the fairgrounds each July, and downtown transforms every October for Scarecrow Madness, when hundreds of hand-built scarecrows line the square for local nonprofits. Add Youngs Creek, an active parks system, and neighbors who actually know one another, and you get a community that feels lived-in rather than staged. For buyers who want more than a bedroom suburb, that texture is the whole point.

Housing Market & Price Ranges

Franklin offers some of the most attractive value in the Johnson County market. Homes typically range from the $180s for smaller starter homes and older properties in established neighborhoods to the upper $340s for updated or newer-construction homes on larger lots. The mix is broad: historic homes near the square and the college draw buyers who prize character and craftsmanship, while modest new development on the city’s edges brings contemporary floor plans and finishes.

Compared with Greenwood and the northern suburbs, Franklin lets your dollar stretch further, which is a big reason it keeps landing on relocation short lists. Schools in Franklin

Franklin is served by Franklin Community Schools, the public district that has educated generations of Johnson County families. Franklin Community High School anchors the system and carries real community pride, with elementary and middle schools feeding into it. Buyers with kids consistently rank the district among the top reasons they choose Franklin over comparable towns.

There is also the college-town dimension. Even a small institution like Franklin College brings cultural events, guest speakers, and athletics that enrich family life. Because attendance areas can shift, our team always confirms exactly which schools serve a given address before you write an offer — a detail that matters more than most buyers expect.

Commuting & Getting Around

Franklin commuters typically take SR-44 to I-65 north, which puts downtown Indianapolis roughly 25 to 30 minutes away in normal traffic. The I-65 interchange near Greenwood ties the area into the wider network, including I-465 for getting around the metro without cutting through the center.

Yes, Franklin sits a little further out than Greenwood — but most buyers find the trade worth it. You give up a few minutes of drive time and gain a genuine downtown, more square footage, and a lower price. Day to day, groceries, dining, and the essentials are all close, so the highway is really just for the trip into the city.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is it like living in Franklin, Indiana?

Living in Franklin means a walkable courthouse square, a college-town cultural calendar, and a relaxed pace, all about 25 to 30 minutes south of downtown Indianapolis. It suits buyers who want genuine community and more space for their money.

How much do homes cost in Franklin, Indiana?

Homes in Franklin typically range from the $180s for smaller or older properties to the upper $340s for updated and newer-construction homes on larger lots. It is one of the more affordable options in Johnson County.

What school district serves Franklin?

Franklin is served by Franklin Community Schools, including Franklin Community High School, plus elementary and middle schools. Franklin College adds a college-town dimension to the community.

How long is the commute from Franklin to Indianapolis?

Most Franklin commuters take SR-44 to I-65 north and reach downtown Indianapolis in about 25 to 30 minutes in normal traffic, with I-465 nearby for getting around the metro.
