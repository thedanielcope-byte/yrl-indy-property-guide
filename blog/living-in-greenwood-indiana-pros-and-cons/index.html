Living in Greenwood, Indiana: Pros and Cons

An honest look at Greenwood: home prices, taxes, schools, the commute, neighborhoods and who it suits best, from a local Johnson County team.

Living in Greenwood at a Glance

Is Greenwood, Indiana a good place to live? Yes, for buyers who want value and a short commute. Greenwood sits about 15 minutes from downtown Indianapolis, homes typically range from the $250s to the $450s, and Old Town and Craig Park keep a busy community calendar. The tradeoffs are freight trains through Old Town, a road project on the north edge that’s still years out, and school boundaries that vary by address, so verify the district before you buy.

Greenwood is the south side’s retail and dining hub, with a revitalized Old Town and one of the quickest commutes in the metro, about 15 minutes to downtown. It offers some of the best value around Indianapolis, but which school district you get depends on the exact street.

Pros of Living in Greenwood

  • Around 15 minutes to downtown Indianapolis via I-65 or US-31, one of the quickest commutes from the south suburbs.
  • Homes typically run from the $250s to the $450s, and parts of the city sit in the sought-after Center Grove school district.
  • Old Town’s Craig Park hosts a summer concert series and the Freedom Festival, and the city runs Freedom Springs aquatic park.
  • Johnson County’s 1.40% local income tax is below Marion County’s 2.02%, roughly $620 a year less per $100,000 of income.

Cons of Living in Greenwood

  • The Louisville & Indiana Railroad freight line runs through downtown, so expect occasional waits at crossings near Old Town.
  • School district depends on the exact address — Center Grove, Greenwood Community or Clark-Pleasant — so check boundaries before you write an offer.
  • Indianapolis plans to widen County Line Road along Greenwood’s northern edge, but construction isn’t expected to start until 2028.

Taxes. Johnson County’s 1.40% local income tax (2026) is below Marion County’s 2.02%, about $620 a year less per $100,000 of income, though Hamilton County’s 1.10% is lower still. On property taxes, Indiana caps the bill on your primary home at 1% of its gross assessed value (not counting voter-approved referendums), and the homestead deduction lowers the taxable value, but only if it’s filed. Neighborhoods and Communities in Greenwood

Popular areas include revitalized Old Town Greenwood, the sought-after Center Grove area, Stones Crossing and the west side and newer subdivisions near Worthsville Road.

We profile 14 Greenwood communities, including Kensington Grove, Enclave at Stones Crossing, Sagebriar by Del Webb, Maxwell Springs, Foxberry Trace, Cobblestone, Willow Lakes and The Villages at Grassy Creek. Browse them all in our communities directory.

Schools in Greenwood

Assignment depends on the address — west-side Greenwood (White River Township) is Center Grove, the established central/older areas are Greenwood Community, and southern/southeastern pockets fall in Clark-Pleasant.

Public school districts: Center Grove Community School Corporation, Greenwood Community School Corporation, Clark-Pleasant Community School Corporation.

Private schools in Greenwood: Our Lady of the Greenwood Catholic School, Greenwood Christian Academy.

School assignment follows the address, so confirm boundaries with the district before you write an offer. Our Central Indiana schools guide covers every district.

Commute and Getting Around

Greenwood is around 15 minutes to downtown Indianapolis via I-65 and US-31. Rush-hour times run longer, so test-drive the commute at the hours you’d actually drive it. Comparing drives? See the best Indianapolis suburbs for commuters.

Things to Do in Greenwood

Here’s a sample of what fills the Greenwood calendar through the year:

  • Greenwood Freedom Festival (each summer, near Independence Day): The Greenwood Freedom Festival fills Craig Park each summer with a parade, live music, a drone show, and fireworks.

Event calendars: Festival Country Indiana events · Johnson County Public Library events · Johnson County Fairgrounds · official Greenwood website.

Who Greenwood Suits

Greenwood suits commuters who want south-side value, lots of shopping and dining, and a quick drive downtown. If you’d rather have a quiet courthouse-square town, look at Franklin; for more brand-new construction, look at Bargersville. Weighing it against the others? See Greenwood vs Franklin vs Zionsville.
