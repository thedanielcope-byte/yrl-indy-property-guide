How to Make a Winning Offer Without Overpaying

You found the one. Here's how to write an offer that gets accepted in Central Indiana — price, contingencies, earnest money, and the terms that actually move a seller.

Buying a Home Making an Offer Central Indiana

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Buying a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~7 min read

Finding the right home is exciting — and then comes the part that makes people nervous: the offer. Offer too low and you lose it; offer too high and you overpay. The good news is that a winning offer is about strategy, not just price. Here's how Central Indiana buyers write offers that get accepted without overpaying. This is Step 2 of the home buying process.

Start With the Right Price

Your agent runs a comparative market analysis (CMA) — recent sales of similar homes nearby — to anchor your offer in reality. In a hot segment you may need to be at or above list; on a home that's lingered, there's room to negotiate. Price is where you start, but it's rarely the only thing that wins.

Understand Your Contingencies

Contingencies are your safety exits: the financing contingency, the inspection contingency, and the appraisal contingency each let you renegotiate or walk away if something goes wrong. Keeping reasonable contingencies protects you; waiving them can make an offer stronger but riskier. We help you decide which are worth keeping for your situation.

Win on Terms, Not Just Price

Sellers care about certainty and convenience, not only the number. A larger earnest money deposit signals you're serious. Flexibility on the closing date or a short rent-back can matter more than a few thousand dollars. A clean, well-documented offer with a strong pre-approval often beats a slightly higher but shakier one.

Be Ready to Negotiate

Most deals involve a counter or two. Decide your ceiling in advance so emotion doesn't take over, and let your agent handle the back-and-forth. In multiple-offer situations, an escalation clause can keep you competitive up to a limit you set. The goal: win the home on terms you're genuinely comfortable with.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much earnest money should I put down?

In Central Indiana, earnest money is commonly around 1% of the purchase price, though a larger deposit can strengthen your offer. It's applied to your down payment or closing costs at closing, and it's protected by your contingencies if the deal falls through for a covered reason.

Should I waive the inspection to win?

Rarely a good idea. You can make your offer competitive in other ways — earnest money, closing flexibility, a clean pre-approval — without giving up your right to inspect. If you do shorten or waive contingencies, understand the risk first; we'll walk you through it.

What is an escalation clause?

It automatically raises your offer by a set amount above competing offers, up to a maximum you choose. It keeps you competitive in a bidding war without blindly overpaying, but it's not right for every situation.

How fast do I need to move in Indianapolis?

In popular price ranges and neighborhoods, desirable homes can get offers within days. Being pre-approved and ready to write lets you act fast — which is exactly why we handle the paperwork so you can move the moment you find the one.
