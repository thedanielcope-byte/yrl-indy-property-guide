Moving from California to Indianapolis: Costs, Taxes & Where to Live

How home prices, rent, taxes and everyday costs really compare, the trade-offs Californians notice first, and where to look in Central Indiana.

Relocation California to Indy Updated for 2026

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Relocation November 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

California vs. Indianapolis at a Glance

Is Indianapolis cheaper than California? Yes, by a wide margin, mostly because of housing. Everyday prices run about 14% lower in the Indianapolis metro than in California overall (BEA, 2024), the typical home is valued at about $305,000 versus $771,000 statewide, and median rent is about $1,380 versus $2,160 (Census, 2025). The trade-offs: real winters, humid summers, flat terrain and a car-dependent metro.

California to Indianapolis is one of the bigger lifestyle changes we help people make: a different climate, a different time zone and a very different housing market. Most people we talk to are moving for space, a lower monthly payment, family or a job, and many keep a California salary and work remotely. Here’s how the costs actually compare, using federal and state data, plus the honest downsides and where to start looking.

Cost of Living: What the Federal Data Shows

The Bureau of Economic Analysis measures how expensive each place is compared with the national average. In 2024 the Indianapolis metro scored 95.7 and California 110.7, so everyday prices run about 14% lower here. Housing is where the gap gets dramatic: the BEA’s housing index is 88.9 for Indianapolis versus 154.3 for California, 170.4 for Los Angeles and 179.3 for San Diego.

Incomes are lower too, which is the honest other half. The median household income is $83,815 in the Indianapolis metro and $102,159 in California (Census, 2025). But home values fall much further than incomes: the typical home is worth about 3.6 times the median household income here, compared with about 7.5 times in California (our math from the same Census tables). If you keep a California-level salary, that difference goes a very long way. Utilities follow the same pattern. Indiana’s average residential electricity price was 17.02¢ per kWh in August 2026, against 29.75¢ in California (EIA). For a fuller breakdown, see our cost of living in Indianapolis guide.

Home Prices and What Your Payment Buys

Across the MIBOR region, the median home sold for $320,000 in September 2026, according to our market update. For a like-for-like comparison with California, the Census Bureau’s 2025 survey puts the median home value at $304,600 in the Indianapolis metro and $770,700 statewide.

Here’s what that means for a monthly payment. At the 7.24% average 30-year rate Freddie Mac reported on October 29, 2026, with 10% down, principal and interest come to about $1,868 a month on a $304,600 home, versus about $4,727 on a $770,700 home (our math, before taxes and insurance). That difference is why many of the Californians we work with buy a newer or larger home here, or keep part of their equity in the bank. To see what your budget buys, use our how much house can I afford guide, then browse our best suburbs for families and most affordable suburbs rankings or search live listings.

Taxes: Income, Property and Sales

Income tax. California’s 2026 rates run from 1% to 12.3%, plus an extra 1% Behavioral Health Services Tax on taxable income over $1 million. The 9.3% rate starts at $75,197 of taxable income for single filers and $150,394 for joint filers (FTB 2026 tax rate schedules). California workers also pay 1.3% of their wages for State Disability Insurance in 2026, with no wage limit (EDD). Indiana has a flat 2.95% state rate plus a county income tax, such as 1.10% in Hamilton County (Carmel, Fishers, Westfield) and 2.02% in Marion County (Indianapolis), or about 4.05% to 4.97% combined. Indiana sets your county rate by where you live or work on January 1, which matters in your move year (Indiana DOR).

How much you save depends on your income. California’s rates on the first $40,000 or so of a single filer’s taxable income are only 1% to 4%, so many middle-income households see a smaller difference than they expect. The gap grows quickly once income reaches the 9.3% bracket and above. Deductions and credits differ between the states, so run your own numbers with a tax professional.

Property tax. Both states cap the tax rate on a home at about 1%. California’s Proposition 13 limits the rate to 1% plus voter-approved debt and limits increases in taxable value to 2% a year, but a home is reassessed at its purchase price when it sells (Legislative Analyst’s Office). So the low bill on a California home you’ve owned for years isn’t the right comparison; the price of your next home is. Indiana caps the tax on your primary home at 1% of its assessed value once you file the homestead deduction (how the caps work). The median homeowner bill is $2,596 in the Indianapolis metro and $5,537 in California (Census, 2025), largely because homes here cost less. See property taxes by county around Indianapolis and how to file the homestead deduction.

Sales tax. California’s statewide base rate is 7.25%, and local district taxes add 0.10% to 2.00% in many areas (CDTFA). Indiana’s sales tax is 7% statewide.

Keeping a California Job

Plenty of our California clients keep working for a California employer after they move. Two things to plan for. First, the clock: Indianapolis is on Eastern time, three hours ahead of California, so a 9 a.m. Pacific meeting is noon here. Second, taxes: the California Franchise Tax Board says nonresidents are taxed only on income from California sources, including pay for work performed in California. In the year you move, you’ll generally file as a part-year resident (California Form 540NR) for the months you lived there. Residency is a question of facts, so have a tax professional review your situation before and after the move.

The Trade-Offs Californians Notice First

  • Winter. January averages 28.5°F, and the area gets about 25.5 inches of snow a year, mostly December through February (National Weather Service, 1991-2020 normals).
  • Humid summers. July highs average about 85°F, and humidity makes it feel warmer.
  • No ocean or mountains. Central Indiana is flat. Locals use reservoirs like Geist and Morse, trails like the Monon and state parks within an hour or so.
  • Driving. Walk Score rates Indianapolis 31 out of 100, so most households drive. The good news is the average commute is 25.1 minutes, below the national 27.4 (Census, 2025).
  • Getting back to California. Indianapolis International has nonstop flights to Los Angeles and San Francisco, plus seasonal nonstops to San Diego and Burbank (airport flight list, November 2026).

For the full picture, read our honest pros and cons of living in Indianapolis.

Where to Look, Based on What You Want

  • Walkable, urban living: close-in neighborhoods like Mass Ave, Fountain Square and Broad Ripple.
  • Newer homes and highly rated schools: Hamilton County suburbs like Carmel, Fishers and Westfield, which also have the metro’s lowest county income tax.
  • Value with a short commute: Greenwood and the south side, including Perry Township.
  • More room and bigger lots: Hendricks County towns like Brownsburg and Danville.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Indianapolis cheaper than California?

Yes. The Bureau of Economic Analysis put 2024 price levels at 95.7 for the Indianapolis metro and 110.7 for California, so everyday prices run about 14% lower. Housing is the biggest difference: the median home value is $304,600 in the Indianapolis metro versus $770,700 in California, and median rent is $1,380 versus $2,160 (Census ACS, 2025).

How much cheaper are houses in Indianapolis than in California?

The typical home is valued at about 60% less. The Census Bureau's 2025 survey puts the median home value at $304,600 in the Indianapolis metro and $770,700 in California, and the median home sold for $320,000 across the MIBOR region in September 2026. At a 7.24% rate with 10% down, principal and interest are about $1,868 a month on a $304,600 home versus about $4,727 on a $770,700 home.

Will I pay less income tax in Indiana than in California?

Usually, though the savings depend on your income. Indiana charges a flat 2.95% plus a county tax, about 4.05% to 4.97% combined in Hamilton and Marion counties. California's 2026 rates run from 1% to 12.3%, plus 1% on taxable income over $1 million, and workers also pay 1.3% for State Disability Insurance. Middle incomes see a smaller gap than high incomes. Check your numbers with a tax professional.

Do I still owe California tax if I keep my California job and work remotely from Indiana?

Generally, California taxes nonresidents only on California-source income, which includes pay for work performed in California, according to the Franchise Tax Board. In the year you move, you typically file a part-year resident return. Residency depends on your facts, so get advice from a tax professional.

What is the weather like in Indianapolis compared with California?

Colder in winter and more humid in summer. January averages 28.5 degrees with about 25.5 inches of snow a year, and July highs average about 85 degrees, based on National Weather Service 1991-2020 normals.

Are there direct flights from Indianapolis to California?

Yes. As of November 2026, Indianapolis International Airport lists nonstop flights to Los Angeles and San Francisco, plus seasonal nonstops to San Diego and Burbank. Schedules change, so check the airport's flight list for current routes.
