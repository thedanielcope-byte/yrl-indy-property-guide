Moving from Chicago to Indianapolis: Costs, Taxes & Where to Live

About three hours down I-65: what the federal data says about cost of living, home prices and taxes, plus the time-zone switch and where Chicago transplants tend to buy.

Relocation Central Indiana By Daniel Cope

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated October 2026

Relocation October 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~7 min read

Every year a steady stream of Chicagoans head about three hours south on I-65 and land in Central Indiana. The draw is easy to see: a lower cost of living, much lower property tax bills, newer and bigger homes for the money, and a metro that’s simple to get around. Here’s what the numbers actually show, using federal, state and county sources, and where Chicago transplants tend to settle.

Cost of Living: What the Federal Data Shows

The Bureau of Economic Analysis measures how expensive each metro is compared with the national average. In 2024 the Indianapolis metro scored 95.7 and the Chicago metro 103.6, so everyday prices in Indianapolis run about 7.6% lower. The biggest gap is housing: 88.9 for Indianapolis versus 112.0 for Chicago, roughly 21% lower. These are metro-wide averages, so individual neighborhoods vary, but the direction is clear.

Home Prices and What Your Budget Buys

The Census Bureau’s 2024 survey puts the median home value at $291,400 for the Indianapolis metro and $339,700 for the Chicago metro. Inside city limits the gap is wider: $241,500 in Indianapolis versus $341,200 in Chicago. In practice, many of our Chicago clients find they can move from a condo or townhome into a detached house with a yard. To see what your budget buys town by town, start with our most affordable suburbs and best suburbs for families rankings, or search live listings.

Taxes: Income, Property and the Trade-Offs

Income tax. Illinois has a flat 4.95% income tax. Indiana’s state rate is 2.95% for 2026, plus a county income tax that depends on where you live: 1.10% in Hamilton County (Carmel, Fishers, Westfield, Noblesville) and 2.02% in Marion County (Indianapolis), for example. That puts the combined rate close to Illinois’s in Marion County and about a point lower in Hamilton County. Exemptions and deductions differ between the states, so run your own numbers with a tax professional. Indiana sets your county rate by where you live on January 1, which matters in your move year.

Property tax. This is where Chicago buyers notice the biggest difference. The median homeowner bill was $2,228 in Marion County versus $6,349 in Cook County (Census ACS 2024). Indiana’s constitution caps property taxes on your primary home at 1% of its assessed value, and you’ll want to file the homestead deduction with the county auditor after you close. Comparing counties? See property taxes by county around Indianapolis.

The Drive, Trips Back and the Time Zone

Indianapolis is about 183 miles from Chicago, roughly three hours, almost all of it on I-65. One thing to plan for: Chicago is on Central time and Indianapolis is on Eastern, so you lose an hour heading south. The northwest Indiana counties near Chicago (Lake, Porter, LaPorte, Jasper, Newton and Starke) stay on Central time, so the switch happens partway down the drive. If you’re keeping a Chicago employer on a hybrid schedule, mind the one-hour calendar offset.

Where Chicago Transplants Tend to Look

  • Walkable, urban living: downtown and close-in neighborhoods like Mass Ave, Fountain Square and Broad Ripple.
  • Amenity-rich suburbs: Carmel, with its Arts & Design District and the Monon Trail, and Fishers, with the Nickel Plate District.
  • Newer construction: Westfield, Whitestown and McCordsville.
  • Value with a short commute: Greenwood and the south side, including Perry Township.

Your Move Checklist

  • Update your driver’s license and vehicle registration with the Indiana BMV.
  • File your homestead deduction with the county auditor after you close.
  • Check which county’s income tax applies for your move year.
  • Set up utilities before move-in with our Central Indiana utilities guide.
  • Talk with us about buyer representation; a written buyer agreement sets out how your agent is paid, so you know the terms before you tour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it cheaper to live in Indianapolis than Chicago?

Yes, on federal measures. The Bureau of Economic Analysis puts 2024 overall price levels at 95.7 for the Indianapolis metro versus 103.6 for the Chicago metro (U.S. = 100), about 7.6% lower, and housing costs about 21% lower.

How far is Indianapolis from Chicago?

About 183 miles, roughly three hours by car, almost all of it on I-65.

Are property taxes lower in Indianapolis than Chicago?

Generally, yes. The median homeowner property tax bill was $2,228 in Marion County versus $6,349 in Cook County (Census ACS 2024), and Indiana caps homestead property taxes at 1% of assessed value.

What time zone is Indianapolis in?

Eastern time. Chicago is on Central time, so you lose an hour driving south; the northwest Indiana counties near Chicago stay on Central time.
