Moving from Columbus to Indianapolis: Costs, Taxes & Where to Live

About three hours west on I-70: how cost of living, home prices and taxes really compare, and where Columbus transplants tend to buy.

Relocation Columbus to Indy Updated for 2026

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Relocation November 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~7 min read

Columbus vs. Indianapolis at a Glance

Is Indianapolis cheaper than Columbus? A little, mostly on housing. Overall prices are about the same (the BEA scores Indianapolis 95.7 and Columbus 95.5, where the U.S. is 100), but homes sell for a bit less in Indianapolis, rent is lower, and the typical property tax bill is about $1,800 a year lower. Income taxes land in the same range, depending on which county you choose. The drive is about 176 miles on I-70.

Columbus and Indianapolis might be the two most alike big cities in the Midwest. Both are state capitals, both metros have about 2.2 million people, and the typical household earns almost exactly the same in each. So a move from Columbus usually isn’t about a dramatically cheaper life. It’s about a job, family, or getting more house for the money. Here’s what the federal, state and local data shows, where Indianapolis comes out ahead, where it doesn’t, and where Columbus transplants tend to settle.

Cost of Living: What the Federal Data Shows

The Bureau of Economic Analysis measures how expensive each metro is compared with the national average. In 2024 the Indianapolis metro scored 95.7 and the Columbus metro 95.5, which is a statistical tie. Goods (94.3 vs. 93.6) and housing (88.9 vs. 87.9) are nearly identical too. The one category with a real gap is utilities: Indianapolis scored 86.4 to Columbus’s 95.5, so utility costs run roughly 10% lower relative to the rest of the country. Incomes match as well: the median household earned $83,815 in the Indianapolis metro and $84,015 in the Columbus metro in 2025 (Census ACS).

In other words, your grocery bill and your paycheck will feel about the same. The differences show up in home prices, rent and, most of all, property taxes. Our cost of living in Indianapolis breakdown goes line by line through rent, utilities, taxes and the income you need.

Home Prices and Rent

Columbus REALTORS® reported a median sale price of $334,250 for September 2026, while the median across the MIBOR area around Indianapolis was $320,000, according to our market update. That’s about $14,000 less in Indianapolis, or roughly $90 a month on a mortgage with 10% down at the current 7.24% rate (Freddie Mac, Oct. 29, 2026). Homes also sold a little faster here: a median of 27 days versus 33. Each association reports on its own service area, so treat this as a rough comparison.

The Census Bureau’s 2025 survey, which asks owners what their homes are worth, shows a wider gap: a median of $304,600 in the Indianapolis metro versus $347,600 in the Columbus metro. Renters see a smaller difference, with median gross rent at $1,380 versus $1,468, about $90 a month. Central Indiana’s fast-growing suburbs also have plenty of newer construction, so many buyers trade up to a newer home at a similar price. Start with our best suburbs for families and most affordable suburbs rankings, see how much house you can afford, or search live listings.

Taxes: Income, Property and the Trade-Offs

Income tax. Ohio’s income tax is a flat 2.75% for 2026 on income above $26,050, and the City of Columbus adds a 2.5% income tax (residents who work in another Ohio city get credit for that city’s tax, up to 2.5%). Indiana’s state rate is 2.95% on nearly all income, plus a county income tax based on where you live on January 1: 1.10% in Hamilton County, 1.40% in Johnson, 1.70% in Hendricks, 1.71% in Boone, 1.94% in Hancock and 2.02% in Marion County. Here’s our rough math for a single filer with $100,000 in wages, before exemptions, deductions and credits:

So income tax is close to a wash. You’ll pay less than in Columbus if you live in Hamilton or Johnson County, about the same in Hendricks or Boone, and a few hundred dollars more in Marion or Hancock County. In your move year, expect to file part-year returns in Ohio and Columbus.

Property tax. This is where Indianapolis clearly wins. The median homeowner property tax bill was $2,596 in the Indianapolis metro in 2025 versus $4,433 in the Columbus metro (Census ACS), about $1,800 a year less, and inside the city limits it was $2,393 in Indianapolis versus $3,900 in Columbus. Indiana’s constitution caps property taxes on your primary home at 1% of its assessed value once you file the homestead deduction. Bills still follow home values: Hamilton County’s median was $4,356, close to Columbus’s, but on homes with a median value of $483,600. Compare counties in our guide to property taxes by county around Indianapolis. This is general information, not tax advice, so run your own numbers with a tax professional.

The Drive and Trips Back

Indianapolis is about 176 miles west of Columbus, almost all of it on I-70, roughly a three-hour drive. Both cities are on Eastern time, so there’s no clock change. I-70 comes into Indianapolis from the east, so if you’ll be driving back to Ohio often, the east side and Hancock County keep that trip shortest.

Where Columbus Transplants Tend to Look

  • If you liked a newer, planned suburb like Dublin or New Albany: Carmel, Fishers and Westfield in Hamilton County, which also has the metro’s lowest county income tax, and Zionsville with its brick-street village.
  • If you liked German Village, the Short North or Clintonville: close-in Indianapolis neighborhoods with older homes and walkable streets, like Fountain Square, Mass Ave, Irvington, Broad Ripple and Meridian-Kessler.
  • If you liked the value of Grove City or Hilliard: newer subdivisions with easy interstate access in Greenwood, Franklin Township, Avon and Plainfield.
  • Close to I-70 for trips back to Ohio: Greenfield, Cumberland and Irvington on the east side.

Your Move Checklist

  • Update your driver’s license and vehicle registration with the Indiana BMV.
  • File your homestead deduction with the county auditor after you close.
  • Check which county’s income tax applies for your move year, and plan on part-year Ohio and Columbus returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Indianapolis cheaper than Columbus, Ohio?

Slightly, mostly on housing and property taxes. The Bureau of Economic Analysis puts 2024 price levels at 95.7 for the Indianapolis metro and 95.5 for Columbus, essentially the same. But the median sale price in September 2026 was $320,000 in the MIBOR area versus $334,250 for Columbus REALTORS, median rent is about $90 a month lower, and the median property tax bill is about $1,800 a year lower in the Indianapolis metro (Census ACS 2025).

How far is Indianapolis from Columbus, Ohio?

About 176 miles, almost all of it on I-70, or roughly three hours by car. Both cities are on Eastern time.

Are income taxes higher in Indianapolis or Columbus?

It depends on the county. A Columbus resident pays Ohio's 2.75% on income above $26,050 plus the city's 2.5% income tax. In Indiana it's 2.95% plus a county tax that ranges from 1.10% in Hamilton County to 2.02% in Marion County. On $100,000 of wages, our rough math before exemptions comes to about $4,530 in Columbus, $4,050 in Hamilton County and $4,970 in Marion County.

Are property taxes lower in Indianapolis than in Columbus?

Generally, yes. The median homeowner property tax bill was $2,596 in the Indianapolis metro in 2025 versus $4,433 in the Columbus metro, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. Indiana caps property taxes on a primary home at 1% of assessed value once you file the homestead deduction.

Which Indianapolis suburbs feel most like Dublin or New Albany?

Buyers who like newer, planned suburbs often look at Carmel, Fishers and Westfield in Hamilton County, or Zionsville in Boone County. If you preferred German Village or the Short North, close-in Indianapolis neighborhoods like Fountain Square, Mass Ave and Irvington have a similar walkable, older-home feel.

Can I buy a home in Indianapolis before I move from Columbus?

Yes, and many relocating buyers do. Our team runs live video tours, gives honest neighborhood and condition read-outs, and coordinates inspections and closing so you can buy while you're still in Columbus. At about three hours away, many buyers also make one in-person trip to see their short list.
