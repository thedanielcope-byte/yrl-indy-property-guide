Moving from St. Louis to Indianapolis: Costs, Taxes & Where to Live

About four hours east on I-70: how cost of living, home prices and taxes really compare, where St. Louis comes out ahead, and where St. Louis transplants tend to buy.

Relocation St. Louis to Indy Updated for 2026

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Relocation November 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

St. Louis vs. Indianapolis at a Glance

Is Indianapolis cheaper than St. Louis? Not overall. The BEA puts both metros just below the national average (Indianapolis 95.7, St. Louis 95.1, where the U.S. is 100), and St. Louis is cheaper on rent and utilities. Indianapolis comes out ahead on property taxes, with a median bill about $600 a year lower, and single-family homes sold for a median $20,000 less in September 2026. The drive is about 244 miles on I-70, and Indianapolis is an hour ahead.

St. Louis and Indianapolis have a lot in common: big Midwest metros on I-70, similar paychecks, and plenty of house for the money compared with the coasts. So a move from St. Louis is usually about a job, family or a fresh start rather than a dramatically cheaper life, and on a few costs St. Louis actually comes out ahead. Here’s what the federal, state and local data shows, where each city wins, and where St. Louis transplants tend to settle around Indianapolis.

Cost of Living: What the Federal Data Shows

The Bureau of Economic Analysis measures how expensive each metro is compared with the national average. In 2024 the Indianapolis metro scored 95.7 and the St. Louis metro 95.1, so St. Louis is slightly cheaper overall. The categories pull in different directions. St. Louis is clearly cheaper on housing (79.0 vs. 88.9) and utilities (69.9 vs. 86.4), while goods cost more there (100.0 vs. 94.3), right at the national average. Incomes are a near match: the median household earned $83,815 in the Indianapolis metro and $83,656 in the St. Louis metro in 2025 (Census ACS).

In other words, day-to-day life costs about the same. Your rent and utility bills may run a little higher in Indianapolis, your shopping a little lower, and the bigger differences show up in property taxes and the kind of home you buy. Our cost of living in Indianapolis breakdown goes line by line through rent, utilities, taxes and the income you need.

Home Prices and Rent

St. Louis REALTORS® reported a median single-family sale price of $340,000 for September 2026, while the single-family median across the MIBOR area around Indianapolis was $320,000, according to our market update. That’s about $20,000 less in Indianapolis, or roughly $120 a month on a mortgage with 10% down at the current 7.24% rate (Freddie Mac, Oct. 29, 2026). Homes sold a little faster here too: a median of 27 days versus 31.

The Census Bureau’s 2025 survey, which asks owners what their homes are worth, points the other way: a median of $304,600 in the Indianapolis metro versus $283,300 in the St. Louis metro. Each REALTOR® association reports on its own service area, and the Census counts every owner in the metro, so the honest read is that home prices are close. Renters will notice a gap: median gross rent is $1,380 in the Indianapolis metro versus $1,232 in St. Louis, about $150 a month more here. Central Indiana’s fast-growing suburbs have a lot of newer construction, so many buyers trade up to a newer home at a similar price. Start with our best suburbs for families and most affordable suburbs rankings, see how much house you can afford, or search live listings.

Taxes: Income, Property and Vehicles

Income tax. Missouri’s top individual rate is 4.7% for 2026, and the City of St. Louis adds a 1% earnings tax on residents, wherever they work, and on nonresidents for work done in the city. On the Illinois side of the metro, the state rate is a flat 4.95%. Indiana’s state rate is 2.95% on nearly all income, plus a county income tax based on where you live on January 1: 1.10% in Hamilton County, 1.40% in Johnson, 1.70% in Hendricks, 1.71% in Boone, 1.94% in Hancock and 2.02% in Marion County. Here’s our rough math for a single filer with $100,000 in wages, at the top or flat rates and before exemptions, deductions and credits (Missouri’s lower brackets on the first slice of income would bring its figures down a little):

So if you live in the City of St. Louis now, almost anywhere around Indianapolis will lower your income tax. From St. Louis County, St. Charles County or the Illinois side, it’s closer to a wash: you’ll pay less in Hamilton or Johnson County and about the same in Hendricks or Marion County. In your move year, expect to file part-year returns.

Property tax. This is where Indianapolis comes out ahead. The median homeowner property tax bill was $2,596 in the Indianapolis metro in 2025 versus $3,187 in the St. Louis metro (Census ACS), about $600 a year less, even though Indianapolis home values ran a bit higher in that survey. The suburban gap is bigger: the median bill was $3,641 in St. Louis County and $3,836 in St. Charles County. Inside the city limits it’s close, at $2,393 in Indianapolis versus $2,269 in St. Louis. Indiana’s constitution caps property taxes on your primary home at 1% of its assessed value once you file the homestead deduction. Bills still follow home values: Hamilton County’s median was $4,356, but on homes with a median value of $483,600. Compare counties in our guide to property taxes by county around Indianapolis.

Your car. Missouri charges personal property tax every year on vehicles you own on January 1, assessed at one-third of their value, and you file a declaration with your county assessor. Indiana doesn’t tax vehicles as personal property. Instead you pay a vehicle excise tax with your registration each year, based on the car’s original price class and its age, plus a county excise or wheel tax in some counties. New residents have 60 days to title and register with the Indiana BMV. This is general information, not tax advice, so run your own numbers with a tax professional.

The Drive and the Time Change

Indianapolis is about 244 miles east of St. Louis, almost all of it on I-70, roughly a four-hour drive. Plan for the clock: St. Louis is on Central time and Indianapolis is on Eastern, so you lose an hour heading east and gain it back driving home. I-70 comes into Indianapolis from the west through Plainfield, so if you’ll be making regular trips back to Missouri, the west side and Hendricks County keep that drive shortest.

Where St. Louis Transplants Tend to Look

  • If you liked an older, walkable suburb like Kirkwood or Webster Groves: tree-lined streets and older homes near a small downtown, like Irvington, Broad Ripple and Meridian-Kessler, or Zionsville with its brick-street village.
  • If you liked Soulard, Lafayette Square or Tower Grove: historic, close-in neighborhoods near downtown, like Fountain Square and Mass Ave.
  • If you liked newer suburbs like Chesterfield, O’Fallon or Wentzville: planned neighborhoods and new construction in Fishers and Westfield, where Hamilton County also has the metro’s lowest county income tax, or Avon and Brownsburg on the west side.
  • Close to I-70 for trips back to Missouri: Plainfield and Avon in Hendricks County.

Your Move Checklist

  • Title and register your vehicles and switch your driver’s license with the Indiana BMV within 60 days.
  • File your homestead deduction with the county auditor after you close.
  • Check which county’s income tax applies for your move year, and plan on part-year Missouri (or Illinois) returns, plus the St. Louis earnings tax if it applied to you.
  • Set up utilities before move-in with our Central Indiana utilities guide.
  • Talk with us about buyer representation; a written buyer agreement sets out how your agent is paid, so you know the terms before you tour.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Indianapolis cheaper than St. Louis?

Not overall. The Bureau of Economic Analysis puts 2024 price levels at 95.7 for the Indianapolis metro and 95.1 for St. Louis, and St. Louis is cheaper on rent and utilities. Indianapolis comes out ahead on property taxes, with a median homeowner bill of $2,596 versus $3,187 in 2025 (Census ACS), and on recent sale prices: the single-family median was $320,000 in the MIBOR area versus $340,000 for St. Louis REALTORS in September 2026.

How far is Indianapolis from St. Louis?

About 244 miles, almost all of it on I-70, or roughly four hours by car. St. Louis is on Central time and Indianapolis is on Eastern time, so Indianapolis is an hour ahead.

Are income taxes higher in Indianapolis or St. Louis?

It depends where you live now. Missouri's top rate is 4.7% for 2026 and the City of St. Louis adds a 1% earnings tax; the Illinois side of the metro pays a flat 4.95%. Indiana charges 2.95% plus a county tax from 1.10% in Hamilton County to 2.02% in Marion County. On $100,000 of wages, our rough math before exemptions is about $5,700 in the City of St. Louis, $4,700 in St. Louis County, $4,050 in Hamilton County and $4,970 in Marion County.

Are property taxes lower in Indianapolis than in St. Louis?

Across the metro, yes. The median homeowner property tax bill was $2,596 in the Indianapolis metro in 2025 versus $3,187 in the St. Louis metro, and $3,641 in St. Louis County, according to the Census Bureau's American Community Survey. Inside the city limits the two are close. Indiana caps property taxes on a primary home at 1% of assessed value once you file the homestead deduction.

Do I pay personal property tax on my car in Indiana?

Not a personal property tax like Missouri's. Indiana charges a vehicle excise tax with your registration each year, based on the vehicle's original price class and its age, and some counties add a county excise or wheel tax. New residents have 60 days to title and register their vehicles with the Indiana BMV.

Which Indianapolis suburbs feel most like Chesterfield or Kirkwood?

Buyers who like newer, planned suburbs like Chesterfield or O'Fallon often look at Fishers and Westfield in Hamilton County, or Avon and Brownsburg in Hendricks County. If you preferred an older, walkable suburb like Kirkwood or Webster Groves, look at Irvington, Broad Ripple, Meridian-Kessler or Zionsville's village.
