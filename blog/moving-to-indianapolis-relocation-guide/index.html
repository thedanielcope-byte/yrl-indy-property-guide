Moving to Indianapolis: A Relocation Guide for Out-of-State Buyers

Relocating to Central Indiana? Here's how the metro is laid out, which suburbs fit which buyers, and how our team helps you buy confidently from a distance.

Relocation Central Indiana By Daniel Cope

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Relocation August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~9 min read

People are moving to Indianapolis in a steady stream, and it's not hard to see why: a genuinely affordable cost of living, a diverse job market, an easy-to-navigate metro, and Midwestern quality of life without coastal price tags. If you're relocating from out of state, the challenge isn't whether Indy is a good move — it's figuring out where in the metro you belong, often before you can visit in person. This guide lays out how Central Indiana fits together and how our team helps remote buyers land in the right place.

Why People Are Moving to Indianapolis

Indianapolis consistently draws relocating families and professionals for a simple reason: your money goes further here. Housing costs a fraction of what comparable homes run on the coasts, and the broader cost of living follows suit. Beyond affordability, the metro offers a diverse economy — health care, life sciences, logistics, tech, advanced manufacturing, and a major sports and events scene — plus a central location within a day's drive of much of the country.

Just as important is how livable it is. Traffic is manageable by big-city standards, the airport is consistently top-rated, and there's a real mix of downtown energy, established neighborhoods, and fast-growing suburbs. For a lot of transplants, Indy delivers the amenities they came from at a price that finally lets them buy.

How the Metro Is Laid Out

Central Indiana is a ring of distinct counties and suburbs around Marion County (the city itself). Where you land usually comes down to schools, budget, and commute:

  • Hamilton County (north) — Carmel, Fishers, Noblesville, Westfield. Top-rated schools, newer construction, and amenity-rich suburbs; the metro's premium tier. See Hamilton County.
  • Johnson County (south) — Greenwood, Franklin, Bargersville. Strong value, quick downtown access, and a fast-growing south side. See Johnson County.
  • Hendricks County (west) — Avon, Brownsburg, Plainfield, Danville. Family-friendly, great for airport and west-side commuters.
  • Boone County (northwest) — Zionsville and Whitestown. Small-town charm, top schools, and rapid growth.
  • Marion County — the city's historic neighborhoods and in-town living, from Broad Ripple to the near-downtown districts.

Schools and Commuting

For families, school districts often drive the whole decision, and Central Indiana has many strong ones — especially across the collar counties. Because attendance boundaries don't always follow city lines, it's worth confirming exactly which schools serve a specific address; our guide to the best suburban school districts is a good starting point.

Commuting is one of Indy's underrated perks. The interstate system — I-465 circling the city, with I-65, I-69, I-70, I-74, US-31, and SR-37 radiating out — keeps most suburb-to-downtown drives in the 20-to-35-minute range. Where you work matters: a north-side job pairs naturally with Hamilton or Boone County, while downtown or south-side workers often prefer Johnson County.

Buying From Out of State

Most of our relocating clients buy before they've spent much time here, and a good process makes that comfortable rather than nerve-wracking. It usually starts with a conversation about your must-haves, budget, and commute, followed by a tailored look at the areas that fit — so you're not guessing from a map a thousand miles away.

From there, our team runs live video tours, gives honest read-outs on neighborhoods and condition, and handles the on-the-ground details you can't from afar. We coordinate inspections, timelines, and closing around your move, and connect you with trusted local lenders. Our relocation services are built specifically for buyers making this move remotely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is Indianapolis a good place to move to?

For most relocating buyers, yes. Indianapolis offers an affordable cost of living, a diverse job market, an easy-to-navigate metro with short commutes, and strong suburban school districts — a lot of quality of life for the price.

Which Indianapolis suburb is best for families?

It depends on schools, budget, and commute. Hamilton County (Carmel, Fishers, Westfield) is the premium, top-schools tier; Johnson, Hendricks, and Boone counties offer strong schools and excellent value. Our team helps you match the right area to your priorities.

How long are commutes in Indianapolis?

Thanks to the I-465 loop and radiating interstates, most suburb-to-downtown commutes run about 20 to 35 minutes. Where you work matters — north-side jobs pair well with Hamilton or Boone County, downtown with Johnson County.

Can I buy a home in Indianapolis before I move there?

Yes, and many of our clients do. Our team runs live video tours, gives honest neighborhood and condition read-outs, and manages inspections, timelines, and closing remotely so you can buy confidently from out of state.
