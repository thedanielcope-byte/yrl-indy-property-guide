New Construction Homes in Westfield, Indiana: Communities, Builders & What to Know

The builder communities selling in Westfield right now, what they start at, and what to know about contracts, inspections, incentives and bringing your own agent.

Where can you buy a new construction home in Westfield? As of November 2026 we confirmed 14 builder communities selling in Westfield on the builders’ own websites, from Lennar, Pulte, Del Webb, Fischer Homes, Estridge and Drees. Townhomes start in the $350s, single-family homes in the $480s, and homes at Chatham Hills start at $1 million or more. If you’re searching for new construction in Westfield, you’ll find plenty of it, and a lot of it changes month to month. Sections sell out, new ones open, and prices move. So we went to each builder’s own website in November 2026 and listed only the communities we could confirm are selling now, with the starting prices the builders post. Here’s who’s building, what homes start at, and what to know before you walk into your first model. For the wider metro, see our hub of new construction communities around Indianapolis.

Why Westfield Keeps Building

Few Indiana cities have grown like Westfield. The Census Bureau counted 30,068 residents in 2010 and 46,410 in 2020. A special census on September 15, 2025 counted 64,407, about 39% more than in 2020, and the Bureau’s separate July 2025 estimate put the city at 66,258. In other words, the population has more than doubled since 2010, and new neighborhoods are how the city made room.

Much of that story starts with Grand Park, the city’s 400-plus-acre youth sports campus with 26 diamonds and dozens of fields. One change to know: 2026 was the eighth and final summer of Colts training camp at Grand Park, and the Colts say camp moves to the Indiana Farm Bureau Football Center in Indianapolis in 2027. The tournaments, trails and the US-31 corridor are what keep drawing buyers. Our Westfield real estate guide covers the city as a whole.

A note on ZIP codes: most of Westfield is 46074, but some newer neighborhoods on the edges carry other mailing ZIPs. Pulte’s Finley Creek, for example, is in Westfield and Westfield Washington Schools but uses a Sheridan 46069 mailing address. Search by city and school district, not ZIP alone.

Townhomes and low-maintenance living

If you want something new without the yard work, Westfield has more choices than most suburbs. Lennar’s East Street Towns and Fischer’s Townes at Grand Park Village both include lawn care and snow removal, and Townes at Grand Park Village sits across the street from Grand Park. Pulte’s Towns at Union is the pick if you want to walk to downtown Westfield’s restaurants and Grand Junction Plaza.

Single-family neighborhoods

Lennar’s Ravinia and Winterburg, Estridge’s Midland and Pulte’s Finley Creek cover most of the middle of the market, from the $480s into the $500s and up. Midland mixes single-family homes, townhomes and villas around a pool, beer garden and event space, and Finley Creek includes a full 9-foot basement, which is not standard in every Westfield community.

Golf and luxury

Fischer’s Wood Wind Landing starts in the $740s with fairway and water views near Wood Wind Golf Club. At Chatham Hills, Estridge’s homes start at $1 million and Fischer’s final homes at Lindley Ridge start around $1.35 million, while Lennar’s Towns at Chatham give townhome buyers a way into the same club.

55 and older

Del Webb’s Kimblewick is in its final section, with ranch homes from the $430s, a 13,000-square-foot clubhouse and more than 4 miles of walking trails. Lennar’s earlier 55+ neighborhood, Osborne Trails, no longer appears among Lennar’s Westfield communities, so homes there are now typically resales. What New Construction Costs in Westfield

Based on what the builders posted in November 2026, here’s the rough ladder: townhomes from the $350s to the $400s, single-family homes from the $480s through the $500s and $700s, and Chatham Hills homes at $1 million and up. That lines up with the $300s-to-$600s range for Westfield as a whole in our city guide, with new builds sitting toward the middle and top of it.

The starting price is rarely the final price. A lot premium for a pond, wooded or walkout lot, structural options like a finished basement or third garage bay, and design-center upgrades can add tens of thousands of dollars. Ask each builder for a fully optioned price on the plan and lot you actually want, and compare it with similar resale homes on the live MLS search. Schools and Property Taxes

Schools. Westfield is served by Westfield Washington Schools, and builders including Lennar, Pulte and Drees list their Westfield communities in the district. The district is growing with the city, so school assignments can change as new buildings open. Confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district before you sign.

Income tax. Westfield is in Hamilton County, where the county income tax is 1.10% for 2026, on top of Indiana’s 2.95% state rate, according to the Indiana Department of Revenue. That’s among the lowest county rates around Indianapolis.

Property tax on a new build. Indiana assesses property as of January 1 and bills the following year, and a home that was only partly built on January 1 is assessed as it stood. That means the first tax bill on a brand-new home is often based on the lot or an unfinished house, and the bill typically climbs once the finished home is assessed. Budget for the full amount, not the first bill, and ask your lender how your escrow will adjust. Once you move in, file the Indiana homestead deduction so your primary home gets the 1% property tax cap. Compare counties in our guide to Indiana property tax caps. This is general information, not tax advice.

How Buying From a Builder Works

The builder’s contract. Most production builders use their own purchase agreement, not the forms used in resale. These contracts often set earnest money and design deposits that can be non-refundable, give the builder flexibility on the completion date, limit what you can change after certain deadlines, and send disputes to arbitration. Read it all before you sign, and have it reviewed. Our builder representation page explains what we look for.

Who the sales agent works for. The friendly sales consultant in the model home represents the builder. Their job is to sell that builder’s homes at that builder’s prices, and they won’t tell you that a resale down the street is a better value or that another community has a stronger incentive.

Inspections on a brand-new home

New doesn’t mean perfect. Production homes are built fast by many different trades, and city inspections check code minimums, not workmanship. Many buyers hire their own home inspector for a pre-drywall inspection (while the framing, wiring and plumbing are still visible), a final inspection before closing, and another check before the builder’s first-year warranty ends. Ask up front whether the builder allows outside inspectors and when; most do, but some limit timing or access.

Bringing Your Own Agent: Register on the First Visit

This is the step buyers most often miss. Policies vary, but builders typically require your agent to register you, or to come with you, on your very first visit to a community. Lennar’s published policy, for example, says your agent must be registered at your first contact and must accompany you on your first visit, in person or virtual. M/I Homes tells Indianapolis agents to be there for the client’s first visit. If you tour a model alone and give your name, you may not be able to add an agent later.

How your agent is paid is set in writing up front. A written Buyer Representation Agreement is required before we tour homes with you, including model homes, and it spells out our compensation. Builders often pay the buyer’s agent compensation, but it varies by builder and community (Lennar notes its co-op commissions vary by community), so we’ll confirm the terms for each community before you commit. Builder Incentives and Preferred Lenders

Incentives are where new construction can beat resale. In November 2026, Estridge’s site advertised up to $25,000 in “Estridge Cash” and rates as low as 4.25% on select Midland homes, with conditions. Other builders run similar offers on quick move-in homes, and Fischer, Lennar and others promote financing through their own preferred or affiliated lenders.

The fine print matters. Incentives are usually tied to using the builder’s lender, closing by a deadline, or a specific home. Get a Loan Estimate from the builder’s lender and from at least one outside lender, and compare the total cost, not just the rate. Ask whether the credit can go toward closing costs, a rate buydown or upgrades, and which helps you most. Our new construction negotiation page covers what’s usually on the table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What new construction communities are in Westfield, Indiana?

As of November 2026, builders' own websites showed homes for sale in Westfield communities including Ravinia, Winterburg, East Street Towns and The Towns at Chatham by Lennar; Finley Creek and Towns at Union by Pulte; Kimblewick by Del Webb (55+); Townes at Grand Park Village, Wood Wind Landing and Lindley Ridge at Chatham Hills by Fischer Homes; Midland and Chatham Hills by Estridge; and Carramore and Belle Crest by Drees Homes. Sections sell out and new ones open often, so check with the builder or our team for current availability.

How much do new construction homes cost in Westfield?

Based on builder websites in November 2026, townhomes start in the $350s, single-family homes start in the $480s, Wood Wind Landing starts in the $740s, and homes at Chatham Hills start at $1 million or more. Those are base prices. Lot premiums, structural options and design upgrades can add tens of thousands of dollars.

Do I need my own agent to buy new construction in Westfield?

You don't have to, but the builder's sales consultant represents the builder, not you. If you want your own agent, register them on your first visit, because many builders typically require it; Lennar's policy, for example, requires your agent to accompany you on your first visit. Builders often pay the buyer's agent compensation, but it varies by builder and community, and a written Buyer Representation Agreement is required before your agent tours with you.

Is there a 55+ new construction community in Westfield?

Yes. Kimblewick by Del Webb was selling its final section in November 2026, with ranch homes starting in the $430s, a 13,000-square-foot clubhouse and more than 4 miles of trails, between 146th and 151st streets near US-31. Lennar's Osborne Trails no longer appears among Lennar's Westfield communities, so homes there are now typically resales.

What school district serves new homes in Westfield?

Westfield Washington Schools. Builders including Lennar, Pulte and Drees list their Westfield communities in the district, and Pulte shows its Finley Creek community there even though homes carry a Sheridan 46069 mailing address. Because the district is growing, confirm the assigned schools for a specific address with the district before you sign.

Should I get a home inspection on a brand-new house?

Yes. City inspections check code minimums, not workmanship. Many buyers hire their own inspector for a pre-drywall inspection, a final inspection before closing, and another check before the first-year builder warranty ends. Ask the builder when outside inspectors are allowed before you sign.
