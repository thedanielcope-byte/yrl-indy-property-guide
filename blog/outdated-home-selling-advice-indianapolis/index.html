Old Home-Selling Advice You Can Safely Ignore What Actually Works Today

The market has changed, and some of the tips your neighbor swears by may be costing you.

Home Selling Smart Strategy Central Indiana

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated August 2026

Selling a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~6 min read

Everyone has advice when you're selling a house: a neighbor, a relative, a coworker who sold once years ago. A lot of it was true at some point, but the way people shop for homes has changed, and clinging to outdated rules can cost you time and money. Here's some of the old wisdom Central Indiana sellers can let go of, starting with the one that trips up the most people, which is guessing at price instead of getting a real home valuation.

Myth: You Have to Wait for Spring

Spring has a reputation as the only time to sell, and there's some seasonal truth to it. But it's not the whole story. What matters more is how much competing inventory is on the market. A well-priced home in a low-inventory month can attract more attention than one lost in a crowded spring rush.

Buyers in Central Indiana are looking year-round, relocating for jobs, growing families, and downsizing. The right time to sell is often when you're ready and the local supply of similar homes is thin, not simply when the calendar says April.

Myth: Price High to Leave Room to Negotiate

The old advice says pad your price so you have room to come down. Today's buyers are informed. They and their agents can see comparable sales instantly, and an overpriced home tends to sit. Homes that sit get stale, and stale listings invite lowball offers.

Pricing at fair market value from day one usually generates more interest and can even spark competing offers. That's why starting with an honest valuation, rather than a hopeful guess, is one of the smartest moves a seller can make.

Myth: Grab the First Offer, or Hold Out for More

There are two opposite bits of old advice here, and neither is a rule. You shouldn't feel forced to accept an offer just because it came first; if the terms are weak, it may not be your best deal. But you also shouldn't reject a strong, well-qualified offer while holding out for a mythical better one that may never come.

The right move depends on the whole offer: price, financing, contingencies, and timing. A good agent helps you read all of it and decide with a clear head rather than a superstition.

Myth: Open Houses Sell Homes, and Skip Repairs by Cutting Price

Open houses have their place, but only a small share of homes actually sell directly because of one. For most sellers, strong online photos and video tours do far more of the heavy lifting, since that's where nearly every buyer's search begins.

Another tired shortcut is slashing the price instead of fixing obvious problems. Buyers still expect a home in reasonable condition, and a low price alone rarely makes up for issues they can see. Frequently Asked Questions

Is spring really the best time to sell?

Not always. Buyer demand relative to available inventory matters more than the season. A well-priced home can do very well outside of spring, especially when competing listings are scarce.

Should I price my home high to leave negotiating room?

Usually not. Overpricing tends to make a home sit, which invites lower offers. Pricing at fair market value from the start typically draws more interest and stronger offers.

Do open houses actually sell homes?

They can help, but only a small percentage of homes sell directly from one. Great online photos and video tours reach far more buyers, since most searches now start online.

Is it better to lower the price or make repairs?

It depends, but buyers expect reasonable condition. Often, addressing visible issues and presenting the home well works better than simply cutting the price. Your agent can help you decide.
