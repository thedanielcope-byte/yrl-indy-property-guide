Selling a House As-Is in Indiana

As-is does not mean no disclosure and no inspection. Here is what selling as-is really involves in Indiana, and how to do it the right way.

Selling a Home Central Indiana By Daniel Cope
Selling a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

If life has handed you a house you need to sell without pouring money into repairs, you are not alone, and you have options. An as-is sale is a common and completely legitimate path in Indiana, whether you inherited a property, you are a landlord who is simply worn out, or the home needs more work than you can take on right now. But as-is is one of the most misunderstood terms in real estate. It does not mean what many sellers assume it means, and getting it wrong can cost you. Here is how it actually works in Indiana.

What Selling As-Is Really Means in Indiana

Selling as-is is a straightforward promise to buyers: you will not be making repairs. You are offering the home in its current condition, and the buyer accepts that they are taking it that way. It signals up front that you are not planning to fix the roof, replace the furnace, or patch the things a buyer might otherwise ask you to address. For the right seller, that clarity is a genuine relief.

What as-is does not do is switch off every other part of a normal sale. It is not a magic phrase that removes your legal responsibilities or strips the buyer of their rights. In Indiana, two things in particular still apply no matter how loudly the listing says as-is: your duty to disclose what you know, and the buyer's right to inspect. Misunderstanding those two points is where as-is sellers get into trouble, so let us take them one at a time.

It also helps to say plainly what as-is is not. It is not a way to unload a home nobody wants, and it is not an admission that something is wrong. Plenty of solid, livable homes sell as-is every year in Central Indiana simply because the owner does not want the hassle of repairs. As-is is a strategy, and used correctly it can make your sale simpler, faster, and far less stressful.

As-Is Does Not Erase Your Disclosure Duty

Indiana law requires most residential sellers to complete a seller's disclosure form, and selling as-is does not exempt you from it. The disclosure asks you to report known material defects, the problems you are actually aware of with the roof, foundation, systems, water intrusion, and more. As-is tells the buyer you will not fix those things. It does not give you permission to hide them.

This distinction matters enormously. If you know the basement takes on water every spring, you disclose it, even in an as-is sale. What you cannot do is stay quiet about a defect you know exists just to get a cleaner sale. Honesty here is not only the law, it is also your protection. A well-documented disclosure, paired with an as-is listing, tells buyers exactly what they are getting and helps shield you from claims down the road.

If you are unsure what counts as a material defect or how to complete the form for an unusual situation, do not guess. Your agent can walk you through it, and for legal specifics it is always wise to confirm with an Indiana real estate attorney. When in doubt, disclose.

Buyers Can Still Inspect the Home

The second surprise for many as-is sellers is that buyers can still order a home inspection. Selling as-is does not remove the buyer's ability to hire an inspector and learn everything they can about the property before closing. What changes is the expectation of what happens next.

In a traditional sale, an inspection often leads to a repair request or a credit. In an as-is sale, the buyer inspects mostly for their own knowledge and peace of mind, understanding that you are not agreeing to fix what they find. They may still choose to walk away if the inspection reveals something they cannot live with, depending on how the contract is written. That is why pricing and honest marketing matter so much, which brings us to the next point.

Because of this, the smart move for an as-is seller is not to discourage inspections but to price and present the home so that what an inspector finds is roughly what the buyer already expected. When there are no ugly surprises, inspections tend to confirm the deal rather than derail it. Transparency up front is what keeps an as-is sale on track from offer to closing.

Who Typically Sells As-Is

People choose as-is sales for all kinds of reasons, and many of them are stressful life moments rather than simple preferences. If you are in one of these situations, know that selling as-is is a normal, respectable way forward, not a sign that anything has gone wrong.

  • Estate and inherited homes: heirs often live out of state or simply cannot take on repairs to a loved one's property.
  • Tired landlords: longtime rental owners who are ready to be done and do not want to renovate a worn unit.
  • Distressed situations: divorce, financial strain, or a move that has to happen quickly.
  • Homes needing major work: properties where the cost of repairs is more than the seller can or wants to spend.

If you are settling an estate, the process carries its own steps and emotions. Our guide to probate and estate home sales in Indianapolis covers that path in more detail. Whatever brought you here, the goal is the same: a clean, honest sale that lets you move on without unnecessary stress.

Pricing an As-Is Home Realistically

The most important decision in an as-is sale is the price, because price is how you attract the right buyer. An as-is home will generally not sell for the same figure as a comparable home that has been updated and is move-in ready. Buyers factor in the work they will have to do, and a realistic price reflects that from the start. Overpricing an as-is home is the fastest way to have it sit on the market and grow stale.

The right way to set that price is with a comparative market analysis, a careful look at what similar homes in similar condition have actually sold for in your area. This is far more reliable than an online estimate, which cannot see that your home needs a new roof or that the kitchen is original. Our post on what your home is worth in Indianapolis explains why a real analysis beats an automated guess.

Priced correctly, an as-is home can move efficiently, because you are speaking directly to buyers who want a project or an investment. Priced too high, it lingers, and a long time on the market often leads to a lower final number than if you had started realistically in the first place.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does selling as-is mean I do not have to disclose problems in Indiana?

No. Indiana still requires most sellers to complete a seller's disclosure and report known material defects. As-is means you will not make repairs, not that you can hide known problems.

Can a buyer still get an inspection on an as-is home?

Yes. Buyers can still hire an inspector to learn about the property. The difference is that you are not agreeing to fix whatever the inspection finds.

Will I get less money selling as-is?

Often an as-is home sells for less than a comparable updated home, because buyers account for the work needed. Pricing it realistically with a market analysis helps you attract serious buyers and avoid a long time on the market.
