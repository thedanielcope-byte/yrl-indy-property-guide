Using Home Equity to Buy Your Next Home

Years in your home may have built more buying power than you think. Here is how to turn Central Indiana equity into your next move.

Selling a Home Central Indiana By Daniel Cope
Selling a Home August 2026 By Daniel Cope, Real Estate Broker — Your Realty Link ~8 min read

If you have owned a home in the Indianapolis area for a while, you may be sitting on more buying power than you realize. That buying power is your home equity, and for a lot of Central Indiana homeowners it has quietly become the key that unlocks the next move, whether that is a bigger house in Fishers, a ranch closer to the grandkids, or a lake place near Geist. In this guide I will explain what equity is, how homeowners here have built it, and the practical ways you can put it to work on your next home.

What Home Equity Really Is

Home equity is simply the difference between what your home is worth today and what you still owe on your mortgage. If your home would sell for a certain amount and your loan balance is lower than that, the gap is your equity. It is real wealth, and it is yours, even though it is tied up in the house until you either sell or borrow against it.

Two forces grow your equity over time. The first is paying down your loan balance, month after month, which slowly shifts more of the home into the owned column. The second is appreciation, meaning the home's value rising over the years. For many Indianapolis-area owners, both have been working in their favor at the same time, which is why so many people are surprised, in a good way, when they find out where they stand.

Because equity is tied to your home's current value, it is not a fixed number you set once. It moves as the market moves and as you pay down your loan. That is why any serious conversation about using equity has to start with a fresh, realistic valuation rather than a figure you remember from your last refinance.

How Indy-Area Homeowners Build Equity

Central Indiana has been a steady, sensible housing market for a long time, and that steadiness is exactly what builds equity. I am not going to throw specific appreciation percentages at you, because those change and get stale fast. What I can tell you is that homeowners across Marion, Hamilton, Johnson, and Hendricks counties who bought and simply stayed put have generally watched their ownership stake grow year over year.

A few ordinary things quietly build equity for Indy-area owners:

  • Regular payments: Each mortgage payment chips away at your principal balance.
  • Time in the home: The longer you own, the more principal you pay and the more appreciation can add up.
  • Smart improvements: Sensible updates can support your home's value at resale.
  • A stable local market: Central Indiana's steady demand has supported values over time.

The encouraging reality for longtime owners is that you may have far more equity than your monthly statement suggests, because that statement shows your loan balance but not your home's current market value. The only way to see the full picture is to look at both sides at once.

Turning Sale Proceeds Into Your Next Down Payment

The most common way homeowners use equity is the most straightforward: you sell your current home, pay off the remaining mortgage, and the proceeds left over become the down payment on your next one. For move-up buyers, this is powerful, because years of built-up equity can translate into a substantial down payment on the next house.

A bigger down payment does more than lower your loan amount. Putting down a healthy chunk can help you avoid private mortgage insurance, which reduces your monthly payment, and it can make your offer more competitive to a seller. In a market where sellers weigh how solid each offer is, walking in with strong equity behind you is a real advantage that a good agent will help you present well.

For sellers stepping up to a larger home, this is often the cleanest path. You are not borrowing against the old house or juggling extra products; you are simply moving your accumulated equity from one property to the next in a single, well-timed transaction.

Sell First or Buy First? The Central Indiana Trade-Off

Here is the question almost every equity-rich homeowner wrestles with: do I sell first or buy first? Both paths are common in Central Indiana, and both have real trade-offs. Selling first means you know exactly how much equity you have to work with and you are not carrying two mortgages, but it can leave you needing a temporary place to live if you cannot line up the timing.

Buying first means you can move once, on your schedule, without a gap, but it can stretch you financially in the window before your current home sells, and it may lean on tools like a bridge loan. There is no universally right answer. The best choice depends on your finances, your timeline, and how the local market is behaving when you make your move. This is exactly the kind of decision where sitting down with an experienced broker pays off, because we can map the timing to your specific situation.

Other Ways to Tap Your Equity

Selling is not the only way to access equity. Some homeowners want to keep their current home, or pull cash for a down payment before they sell, and there are lending tools designed for that. I am going to keep this general and stay away from quoting rates or specific terms, because those move constantly and depend on your lender and your profile. At a high level, homeowners commonly consider two routes:

  • A home equity line of credit: A revolving line secured by your home that you can draw against as needed.
  • A cash-out refinance: Replacing your existing mortgage with a larger one and taking the difference in cash.

Each of these has costs, risks, and qualification requirements, and the right fit depends entirely on your numbers. Talk with a trusted lender about current options, and loop in your agent so the strategy lines up with your actual buying and selling plan.

Start by Knowing Your Number

Every one of these strategies starts with the same first step: knowing your number. You cannot plan a move, size a down payment, or decide between selling first and buying first until you have a realistic, current picture of what your home is worth. Guessing high leads to disappointment, and guessing low can cost you thousands in buying power you did not know you had.

The best way to get that number is a comparative market analysis from a local agent who actually knows your neighborhood, not an automated estimate that has never set foot in your home. If you want to understand the difference, our post on CMA versus Zestimate in Indianapolis breaks it down, and our guide on what your home is worth in Indianapolis goes deeper. Keep in mind that a lender's home appraisal later in the process is a separate, formal valuation.

Knowing your number is free, it is low-pressure, and it turns a vague sense of maybe we could move into a concrete, confident next step.

Before you fall in love with your next house, get a real, local read on your current one. Your equity is the foundation of the whole move, and knowing it turns wishful thinking into a workable plan.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know how much equity I have in my Indianapolis home?

Start with a comparative market analysis from a local agent to estimate your home's current value, then subtract your remaining mortgage balance. That gap is your approximate equity.

Should I sell my current home before buying the next one?

It depends on your finances and timing. Selling first gives you certainty about your equity and avoids two mortgages, while buying first lets you move once. A local broker can help you weigh the trade-offs.

Can I use my equity without selling my home?

Yes, tools like a home equity line of credit or a cash-out refinance let you tap equity while keeping the home. Terms vary, so talk with a trusted lender about current options.
