What County Is It In? Indianapolis-Area Towns by County

Every city and town around Indianapolis by county, plus the neighborhoods people mistake for towns, the county seats and why the county matters when you buy.

Relocation 17 Counties Updated November 2026

✔ Reviewed by Janet Giles-Schultz, Principal Broker · MIBOR member · Updated November 2026

Quick Answer: Which County Is It In?

What county is Fishers, Indiana in? Hamilton County, along with Carmel, Westfield and Noblesville, which is the county seat. Zionsville is in Boone County, Greenwood is in Johnson County and Avon is in Hendricks County. Broad Ripple isn’t a town at all: it’s an Indianapolis neighborhood in Marion County. “What county is that in?” is one of the most common questions we get from people moving to Central Indiana, and it’s a fair one. Fishers and Indianapolis share a border but not a county, Broad Ripple sounds like a town but isn’t one, and a few towns sit in two counties at once. Below is every incorporated city and town in the 17 counties our team covers, grouped by county, with the county seats marked and links to our guide for each place. The town lists come from the U.S. Census Bureau, and the county seats from the State of Indiana. If you’re still choosing where to live, start with our Indianapolis relocation guide.

Three seats trip people up because they aren’t the county’s biggest town. Plainfield is not the Hendricks County seat; Danville is, even though Plainfield (about 38,600 residents) and Brownsburg (about 34,100) are both larger than Danville (about 13,400), according to Census Bureau estimates for 2025. In Jackson County, Seymour (about 22,500) is the biggest city, but the seat is Brownstown (about 3,200). And in Jennings County, the seat is the town of Vernon (about 230), not North Vernon (about 6,600).

Marion County: Indianapolis and Unigov

Indianapolis and Marion County have run as one consolidated government, known as Unigov, since January 1, 1970. Unigov grew the city from about 82 square miles to 402 and made almost all of the county part of Indianapolis, with one mayor elected countywide and a single City-County Council (Encyclopedia of Indianapolis). Three things were left out of the merger, and they still matter when you buy:

  • Excluded cities. The cities of Beech Grove, Lawrence and Southport and the town of Speedway kept their own governments. They’re in Marion County but not part of the City of Indianapolis, although their residents still vote for the mayor and City-County Council and pay countywide taxes. Southport only qualified because it annexed enough land to meet the population requirement just before Unigov took effect.
  • Included towns. Eleven towns are part of the consolidated city but keep a town government with limited powers.
  • Townships and schools. All nine townships remain separate governments, and eleven public school districts still operate inside the county.

One change is coming: under a 2025 state law, Cumberland’s Marion County side leaves the consolidated city and becomes an excluded town on January 1, 2027. Cumberland stays a town, and the change puts zoning, permits and code enforcement for its Marion County side in the town’s hands (Town of Cumberland).

Indianapolis Neighborhoods People Mistake for Towns

Plenty of names you’ll see on listings are neighborhoods of Indianapolis, not separate towns. Every one of these is in Marion County and part of the City of Indianapolis (Geist and Camby also spill into neighboring counties). A few really were towns once:

So the answer to “what county is Broad Ripple in?” is Marion County: it’s an Indianapolis neighborhood in Washington Township, and it hasn’t been a separate town since 1922 (Encyclopedia of Indianapolis). Geist is the one name on this list that crosses county lines. Geist Reservoir sits mainly in northeast Indianapolis and southeast Fishers, so it touches Marion and Hamilton counties, with small portions reaching into Hancock County near Fortville and McCordsville. Our Geist Reservoir guide has more.

Southport is the opposite case: it’s a real city with its own government, but a small one, with about 2,100 residents. The Southport name is used much more widely across the south side, so a home described as “in Southport” may actually be in Indianapolis’s Perry Township. The property record tells you which.

Township names cause their own mix-ups. Franklin Township is an Indianapolis township in the southeast corner of Marion County, while the city of Franklin is the Johnson County seat. Lawrence Township includes the city of Lawrence but also Indianapolis neighborhoods such as Castleton and the Marion County side of Geist. Marion County’s nine townships are Center, Decatur, Franklin, Lawrence, Perry, Pike, Warren, Washington, Wayne, and each link goes to our area guide.

If you’re buying in one of these towns, check the county for the exact address. It changes the property tax rate, the county income tax and which county offices you deal with, and in Cumberland’s case, whether you’re on the Indianapolis side of the line until 2027.

Why the County Matters When You Buy

Property taxes

Indiana sets property tax rates by taxing district, which the Department of Local Government Finance defines as all the local units (county, township, city or town, school corporation, library and others) that serve the same area. Two homes in the same county, or even the same town, can carry different rates:

Source: Indiana DLGF, 2026 certified tax rates by district (gross rates, rounded). Bills are figured on assessed value after deductions, minus credits.

A higher rate doesn’t raise every bill one-for-one, because Indiana caps a primary home’s tax bill at 1% of its assessed value once you file for the homestead deduction (2% for rentals and other residential property). That cap is why the homestead deduction is worth filing right after you close, and our guide to Indiana’s property tax caps explains how it works. The rate still matters for rentals and second homes, and in districts where the bill lands under the cap.

County income tax

Every Indiana county adds its own income tax on top of the state’s, based on the county you live in on January 1. Around Indianapolis, the 2026 rates range from 1.10% in Hamilton County to 2.72% in Morgan County (Indiana Department of Revenue, rates effective October 1, 2026):

On a $100,000 household income, the gap between Hamilton and Marion counties is roughly $900 a year before exemptions. Our cost of living breakdown puts that next to rents and home prices.

County offices, schools and vehicles

  • Homestead deduction: you file it with the county auditor, and filing by January 15 gets it onto that year’s tax bills. Our homestead guide walks through it.
  • Assessed value: your property record and assessed value come from the county assessor’s office, and appeals start there too (see how to appeal your assessment).
  • Schools: school districts follow their own boundaries, not county or town lines. Marion County alone has eleven. Confirm the district for the exact address with our Central Indiana schools guide.
  • Vehicles: some counties add a county excise surtax or wheel tax when you register a vehicle, so your county affects what you pay at the BMV. Our moving to Indiana checklist covers the BMV deadlines.

How to Check the County for Any Address

Don’t rely on the mailing address. The city name the Postal Service assigns doesn’t always match the town or county a home is in, and names like Camby, Geist and Southport are used across several jurisdictions. The reliable check is the county’s own property record: search the address on the county assessor’s or GIS site (the State of Indiana’s county map links to every county’s website), and the record will show the county, township and taxing district. If you’re looking at a specific home, our team can check it with you before you make an offer.

Frequently Asked Questions

What county is Fishers, Indiana in?

Fishers is in Hamilton County, just northeast of Indianapolis. Carmel, Westfield and Noblesville are in Hamilton County too, and Noblesville is the county seat. The city of Fishers lies entirely within Hamilton County.

What county is Zionsville, Indiana in?

Zionsville is in Boone County, northwest of Indianapolis. Lebanon is the Boone County seat, and Whitestown, Thorntown and Jamestown are also in Boone County. Zionsville is a town, not a city, even though it has more than 30,000 residents.

What county is Broad Ripple in?

Broad Ripple is in Marion County. It's a neighborhood of the City of Indianapolis in Washington Township, not a separate town. It was its own town from 1884 until Indianapolis annexed it in 1922.

What is the county seat of Hamilton County, Indiana?

Noblesville is the county seat of Hamilton County. The county's courthouse and most county offices are there, even though Carmel and Fishers are larger cities.

Is Plainfield the county seat of Hendricks County?

No. Danville is the county seat of Hendricks County. Plainfield is the county's largest town, with about 38,600 residents in 2025, but it isn't the seat.

Is Beech Grove part of Indianapolis?

Beech Grove is in Marion County but isn't part of the City of Indianapolis. It's one of four excluded communities under Unigov, along with Lawrence, Southport and Speedway, that keep their own governments. Their residents still vote for the Indianapolis mayor and City-County Council and pay countywide taxes. Cumberland's Marion County side joins them on January 1, 2027.
