Coatesville is a small rural town in Clay Township on US-36 in western Hendricks County, founded in the late 1860s and incorporated in 1909. Its defining historical event was the Good Friday tornado of March 26, 1948, which destroyed much of the town; the community rebuilt and today keeps landmarks like the Coatesville-Clay Township Public Library. It sits on the far western edge of the Indianapolis metro, offering an affordable, country setting with older homes and acreage, and is served by the Cascade schools of Mill Creek Community School Corporation. Buyers here are typically drawn by low prices, small-town quiet, and rural land rather than by a large active market. Living in Coatesville, Indiana

Coatesville offers the kind of Hendricks County lifestyle that keeps buyers interested — affordable, rural western-Hendricks town with acreage options, cascade schools (Mill Creek Community School Corporation) in nearby Clayton, small, thin housing market on the far-west edge of the metro. Everyday shopping, dining, parks, and schools are close by, and the surrounding area adds even more. It's a community that suits families and professionals looking for Coatesville-area value with metro access.

Commute from Coatesville to Indianapolis

Coatesville is about 35 miles from downtown Indianapolis, roughly a 45-minute drive east on US-36 through Danville, connecting to I-74 and I-465. Main routes serving the area include US-36, I-74, I-465 (via Danville). For buyers who want Hendricks County value with access to the wider Indianapolis metro, Coatesville is a practical choice.
