Homecroft is a tiny residential enclave in southern Marion County, laid out in 1923 by Frank Gates and incorporated in 1949; it became an 'included town' with its own town council and police under Indianapolis' Unigov consolidation in 1970. The town of Southport borders it directly to the south, and a large portion of Homecroft is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Homecroft Historic District. Housing is almost entirely early-20th-century and mid-century single-family homes on established lots, which draws buyers who want small-town identity and mature streets inside the Indianapolis city limits. It is served by Perry Township Schools (Homecroft Elementary, Southport Middle, Southport High). Living in Homecroft, Indiana

Homecroft offers the kind of Marion County lifestyle that keeps buyers interested — historic small-town enclave on the National Register (Homecroft Historic District), its own town government and police department within Indianapolis under Unigov, established mid-century and early-1900s homes, walkable to Southport. Everyday shopping, dining, parks, and schools are close by, and the surrounding area adds even more. It's a community that suits families and professionals looking for Homecroft-area value with metro access.

Commute from Homecroft to Indianapolis

Homecroft is about 9 miles from downtown Indianapolis, roughly a 15 to 20 minute drive via Madison Avenue (former US-31) or nearby I-65. Main routes serving the area include Madison Ave, US-31, I-65.
