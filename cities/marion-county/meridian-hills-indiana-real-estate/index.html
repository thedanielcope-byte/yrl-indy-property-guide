Meridian Hills is a small, affluent incorporated town in Washington Township about 8 miles north of downtown Indianapolis, strung along the prestigious North Meridian Street corridor. A wooded residential enclave of mostly non-HOA single-family homes, it grew from early-1920s construction, incorporated in 1937, and later became an 'included town' under Unigov. The community is anchored by Meridian Hills Country Club, which opened in 1923, and sits beside Park Tudor School, one of the state's top private schools. Buyers are drawn by mature-tree estate lots, north-side prestige, and Washington Township's North Central High School. Living in Meridian Hills, Indiana

Meridian Hills offers the kind of Marion County lifestyle that keeps buyers interested — affluent, wooded 'included town' about 8 miles north of downtown along North Meridian Street, home to Meridian Hills Country Club (1923) and next to Park Tudor School, established estate homes on large, non-HOA wooded lots. Everyday shopping, dining, parks, and schools are close by, and the surrounding area adds even more. Schools in Meridian Hills, Indiana

Meridian Hills is served by Metropolitan School District of Washington Township (North Central High School); one block lies in Indianapolis Public Schools. School district boundaries are an important factor for many buyers, and assignment depends on the exact address — a Your Realty Link agent can help you identify the right neighborhoods based on your preferred school assignment.

Commute from Meridian Hills to Indianapolis

Meridian Hills is about 15 minutes (roughly 8 miles) from downtown Indianapolis via North Meridian Street (US-31) or Keystone Avenue. Main routes serving the area include North Meridian Street (US-31), Keystone Avenue, I-465. For buyers who want Marion County value with access to the wider Indianapolis metro, Meridian Hills is a practical choice.
