Greenwood vs Franklin vs Zionsville: Which Is Better?

Three towns buyers often weigh together — a south-side retail hub, a historic county seat and a premium northwest village — compared on price, schools, commute and feel.

📍 Johnson & Boone Counties 🏡 $200s – $1M+ 🎓 Three School Districts

Greenwood, Franklin and Zionsville come up together more often than you’d expect, even though they sit on opposite sides of Indianapolis. All three offer something buyers want from a town near the city — convenience, character or top schools — but they deliver it at very different price points. Here’s how they compare, using the same ranges our team uses on each town’s page.

Location and Commute

Start with the map. Greenwood and Franklin sit about 15 minutes apart on the south side along I-65, while Zionsville is on the northwest side in Boone County. If your job is downtown or on the south side, Greenwood wins on drive time. If you work on the north or northwest side, Zionsville’s I-65 and Michigan Road access makes more sense. Franklin asks the most of a daily downtown commuter, at about 30 minutes.

Price: What Your Budget Buys

Greenwood and Franklin overlap from the $250s into the $400s, so you can compare like-for-like homes, and Franklin reaches down into the $200s. Zionsville typically starts in the mid-$400s and climbs past $1 million, so a budget under that line will feel tight there. Our agents can pull recent sales in all three towns so you can compare real numbers for the kind of home you want.

Schools

Zionsville Community Schools are among the state’s top-rated, and that’s a big part of Zionsville’s price. Greenwood spans more than one district — parts of the city are in the sought-after Center Grove district, others in Greenwood Community or Clark-Pleasant — so confirm boundaries by address. Franklin is served by Franklin Community Schools.

Small-Town Feel

Each town reads “small town” differently. Greenwood gives you the most shopping and dining plus a revitalized Old Town. Franklin offers a true county-seat square, the Artcraft Theatre and a college-town rhythm. Zionsville pairs a brick-street Village with larger, often wooded lots.

How to Choose

  • Choose Greenwood if a short commute and everyday convenience matter most.
  • Choose Franklin if you want the most house for the money and a genuine small-town square, and the longer drive works for you.
  • Choose Zionsville if schools and a village setting come first and your budget starts in the mid-$400s.

Before you decide, check the details that are easy to miss: confirm school boundaries by address, factor in county income tax (1.40% in Johnson County versus 1.71% in Boone County), and drive each commute at rush hour. Frequently Asked Questions — Greenwood vs Franklin vs Zionsville

Which is better: Greenwood, Franklin or Zionsville?

It depends on your budget and where you work. Greenwood has the shortest commute (about 15 minutes to downtown) and the most shopping, with homes from the $250s to the $450s. Franklin is the small-town, courthouse-square pick from the $200s to the $400s, with a longer drive. Zionsville offers top-rated schools and a brick-street village, but prices start in the mid-$400s.

Which of the three is the most affordable?

Franklin, with homes typically from the $200s to the $400s, followed closely by Greenwood ($250s to $450s). Zionsville is the premium option, typically from the mid-$400s to over $1 million.

Which has the shortest commute to downtown Indianapolis?

Greenwood, at about 15 minutes via I-65 or US-31. Zionsville is about 25 minutes via I-65 and Michigan Road, and Franklin about 30 minutes via I-65 or US-31.

Do property taxes differ between the three?

All three get Indiana's 1% homestead property tax cap. The bigger difference is local income tax: Johnson County (Greenwood and Franklin) is 1.40%, while Boone County (Zionsville) rose to 1.71% on October 1, 2026.

Sources: price ranges and commutes from our town pages; county income tax rates from the Indiana Department of Revenue (Boone County’s October 2026 change per the American Payroll Association).
