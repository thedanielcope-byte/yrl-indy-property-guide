Quick Answer

How far does a salary go in Indianapolis? Prices in the Indianapolis metro run 4.3% below the national average, and housing (rents) runs 11.6% below (BEA, 2024). So $100,000 in the San Francisco metro buys about what $82,800 does here, and $100,000 in Chicago about what $92,400 does. Pick your own metro and salary below.

    Indianapolis vs. the Suburbs: Costs Across Central Indiana

    BEA prices the whole metro as one number, but what you pay inside Central Indiana changes a lot from county to county. Home prices, local income tax and property tax rates all shift when you cross a county line, and they often pull in opposite directions: Marion County (Indianapolis) had a median sale price of $249,900 in October 2026 against $474,000 in Hamilton County, but its 2026 local income tax is 2.02% against Hamilton’s 1.10%. Across all 17 counties in MIBOR’s region, the median was $310,000 in October 2026, down 1.6% from a year earlier.

    How This Calculator Works

    The data. The U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis publishes Regional Price Parities (RPPs) for every metro area, plus the nonmetropolitan (rural and small-town) part of each state. Each one compares the local price level with the national average, set at 100. Indianapolis, which BEA defines as the Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood, IN metro, scores 95.7 for 2024, so prices there run 4.3% below average. Categories use the same scale, so the U.S. figure for rents, for example, is 100.6 rather than exactly 100; every comparison uses the same category on both sides. This page uses the 2024 figures, the latest available, released February 19, 2026 (BEA tables MARPP for metro areas and PARPP for nonmetro areas). Metro areas follow OMB Bulletin 23-01 (July 21, 2023).

    The math. Equivalent salary = your salary × (price level where you’re going ÷ price level where you are). For a move from the San Francisco metro (115.6): $100,000 × 95.7 ÷ 115.6 ≈ $82,800. Category differences use the same ratio for each category.

    The categories. Housing is based on rents (BEA estimates housing price levels from tenant-occupied homes). Goods covers things you buy, such as food, clothing and household items. Utilities means electricity, gas, water and sewer. Other services is everything else, such as medical care, recreation and transportation services.

    What it leaves out. Taxes aren’t part of RPPs, and Indiana’s flat 2.95% income tax plus county rates, a 7% sales tax and capped property taxes can change the picture a lot (our cost of living breakdown covers them). The figures are metro-wide averages, so a specific neighborhood can cost more or less, home purchase prices aren’t measured directly, and your own spending mix may differ from the average household’s. Prices have moved since 2024, though the gaps between metros tend to change slowly. These are estimates for planning, not financial advice. The cleaned data behind the calculator is available as JSON.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How does the cost of living in Indianapolis compare with the national average?

    The Bureau of Economic Analysis puts prices in the Indianapolis-Carmel-Greenwood, IN metro at 95.7 for 2024, where the U.S. average is 100, so overall prices run 4.3% below average. Compared with the U.S. figure for each category, housing (rents) is 11.6% below, utilities 12.3% below, goods 5.3% below and other services 1.0% below.

    What salary do I need in Indianapolis to match my current one?

    Multiply your salary by the Indianapolis price level (95.7) and divide by your current area's. For example, $100,000 in the New York metro (112.6) works out to about $85,000 in Indianapolis, and $100,000 in the Los Angeles metro (113.6) to about $84,300. The calculator on this page does the math for every U.S. metro area and each state's nonmetro areas.

    Is Indianapolis cheaper than Chicago?

    Yes. In 2024, overall prices were 7.6% lower in the Indianapolis metro than in the Chicago metro, and housing (rents) was 20.6% lower in Indianapolis, according to BEA. Utilities were about 3.3% higher in Indianapolis. $100,000 in Chicago works out to about $92,400 in Indianapolis.

    What does this cost of living calculator leave out?

    Taxes, your own spending habits and differences within a metro. Regional Price Parities measure what goods and services cost, not income, sales or property taxes, which vary by state and, in Indiana, by county. Housing is based on rents rather than home purchase prices, and every figure is a metro-wide average for 2024. Treat the result as a planning estimate, not financial advice.

    Is it cheaper to live in Indianapolis or the suburbs?

    It depends on which costs matter most to you. In October 2026, the median sale price was $249,900 in Marion County (Indianapolis) and $474,000 in Hamilton County, according to MIBOR. But Marion County's 2026 local income tax is 2.02%, compared with 1.10% in Hamilton County. The Central Indiana table on this page compares the surrounding counties side by side.

    How often is this data updated?

    BEA publishes Regional Price Parities once a year. The 2024 figures, the latest available, were released on February 19, 2026. The Central Indiana home prices come from MIBOR's monthly reports; this page shows October 2026.
