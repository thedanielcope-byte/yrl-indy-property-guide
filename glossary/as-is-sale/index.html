What Is an As-Is Sale?

Sold in current condition — but you still have more rights than you might think.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

An as-is sale means the seller is offering the home in its current condition and doesn't plan to make repairs or give credits for them. It does not mean you give up your right to inspect the property. In Indiana, sellers still generally have to complete a seller's disclosure, and buyers can usually still walk away if an inspection reveals a problem.

What "As-Is" Really Means

Selling a home "as-is" signals that the seller won't be making repairs or improvements before closing and won't negotiate credits for the property's condition. The price often reflects that. It's a way of saying, "what you see is what you get."

Importantly, as-is refers to condition, not disclosure. In Indiana, sellers of most residential homes are still required to complete a seller's disclosure form and can't knowingly conceal or misrepresent defects they're aware of.

You Can Still Inspect

An as-is sale doesn't take away your right to a home inspection. Most buyers should still hire an inspector to understand exactly what they're buying. Depending on how your offer is written, you may keep an inspection contingency that lets you cancel if the inspection turns up something you can't live with.

The difference is expectation: in an as-is deal, you generally can't ask the seller to fix what the inspection finds. You can, however, use that information to decide whether to move forward or walk away.

Why Sellers Choose As-Is

Sellers choose as-is for many reasons. Estate and probate sales, where heirs are settling a loved one's property, are often as-is because no one has the time or funds to make repairs. Foreclosures and investor flips are commonly as-is too, as are situations where a seller simply wants a faster, simpler sale.

An as-is listing isn't a red flag by itself — it just tells you the seller's starting position on repairs. Plenty of solid homes are sold this way.

Tips for Buyers and Sellers in Indiana

If you're buying as-is, budget for potential repairs, read the disclosure closely, and lean on your inspection. If you're selling as-is, know that pricing and honest disclosure matter more than ever — a fair price and full transparency attract serious buyers.

Frequently Asked Questions — As-Is Sale

Can I still get an inspection on an as-is home?

Yes. As-is refers to the seller not making repairs, not to your right to inspect. Most buyers should still hire an inspector so they fully understand the home's condition before closing.

Does "as-is" let the seller hide problems?

No. In Indiana, sellers of most homes must still complete a seller's disclosure and can't knowingly conceal known defects. As-is only means they won't repair those issues.

Why would someone sell a house as-is?

Common reasons include estate or probate sales, foreclosures, investor properties, or a seller who wants a quicker, simpler transaction without negotiating repairs.
