What Is Assessed Value?

The number your county uses to calculate your property tax bill, and it's often not the same as what your home would actually sell for.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Every county in Indiana assesses property annually or periodically to determine each home's value for tax purposes. This figure is separate from market value, which reflects what a buyer would actually pay in today's market, so don't be surprised if the two numbers don't match.

How Assessed Value Is Determined

County assessors use standardized valuation methods, comparable sales data, and periodic reassessments to arrive at each property's assessed value. Indiana law requires assessments to reflect market value-in-use, but the timing and methodology mean the figure can lag behind actual market conditions, especially in fast-moving neighborhoods.

Assessed value gets combined with your local tax rate and applicable deductions to calculate your property tax bill. For specific dollar figures on deductions or exemptions, your county auditor's office or the Indiana Department of Local Government Finance (DLGF) is the accurate source, since these amounts can change.

Why the Gap Between Assessed and Market Value Matters

In a rising market, assessed value often trails behind what homes are actually selling for, which is common and doesn't mean anything is wrong. In a cooling market, the opposite can happen.

If a homeowner believes their assessed value is inaccurate, most Indiana counties offer a formal appeal process. Indiana's constitutional property tax caps also limit how much of a homeowner's assessed value can go toward tax, which is worth understanding alongside your specific assessment; see property tax caps for how that protection works.

Frequently Asked Questions — Assessed Value

How often is assessed value updated in Indiana?

Counties reassess periodically and may adjust values annually through trending; check with your county assessor for the current schedule.

Can I appeal my assessed value?

Yes, Indiana counties have a formal appeal process if you believe your assessment doesn't reflect your property accurately.

Does a higher assessed value mean my home is worth more on the market?

Not necessarily. Assessed value and market value are calculated differently and can diverge, especially in a shifting market.

Where can I find my current assessed value?

Your county assessor's or auditor's website typically has a property search tool with your current assessment on file.
