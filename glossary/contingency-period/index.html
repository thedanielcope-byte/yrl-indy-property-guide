What Is a Contingency Period?

The clock every contingency runs on, and the deadline that decides whether you can still walk away.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Every contingency in your contract runs on its own clock, and the contingency period is that clock. Miss the deadline for the inspection contingency, for example, and you may lose the right to negotiate repairs or walk away for that reason, even if you never finished the inspection.

What it is
The deadline attached to a contract contingency
Common lengths
Set per contingency; negotiated case by case
What happens at expiration
The related right to cancel or negotiate can end
Can it be extended
Yes, with written agreement from both sides

Why the Deadline Matters

A contingency only protects you while its period is open. Once it passes without action, in most contracts that contingency is treated as satisfied or waived, and you lose the leverage it gave you.

This is why your agent tracks these dates closely from the moment the purchase agreement is signed; a missed inspection deadline can mean forfeiting your right to ask for repairs at all.

Extending a Contingency Period

If you need more time, for example your lender needs an extra week for underwriting, both parties can agree in writing to extend the period. Neither side can unilaterally extend it on their own.

Sellers aren't obligated to grant an extension, so requesting one early, before the original deadline passes, gives you the best chance of a yes.

Frequently Asked Questions — Contingency period

What happens if I miss my inspection contingency deadline?

In most cases you lose the right to cancel or negotiate based on inspection findings for that contingency once the period closes.

Can a contingency period be shortened to make an offer more attractive?

Yes, buyers sometimes shorten contingency periods to compete in a multiple-offer situation, though it means less time to act.

Who tracks these deadlines?

Your agent and the transaction coordinator typically track every contingency deadline, but it's worth keeping your own calendar too.

Does every contingency have the same period?

No, each contingency, like financing or inspection, is usually given its own separate deadline in the contract.
