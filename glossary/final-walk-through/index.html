What Is a Final Walk-Through?

Your last look before the keys are yours.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A final walk-through is your last look at the home right before closing day. It's not another inspection — it's a chance to confirm the house is in the condition you agreed to, that any negotiated repairs are done, and that nothing has changed since your offer. It usually happens within a day or two of closing.

What You're Checking For

During the walk-through, you're confirming the home is in the shape you expect. Check that agreed-upon repairs were completed, that fixtures and appliances included in the sale are still there, and that the seller has removed their belongings and any trash. Test the basics — lights, faucets, toilets, garage door, heating and cooling, and outlets.

Also look for anything new: damage from moving out, a leak that appeared since your last visit, or items that were supposed to convey but are missing. Bring your purchase agreement and inspection notes so you can compare.

How the Walk-Through Fits the Timeline

The final walk-through is usually scheduled within a day or two of closing, once the seller has moved out. It's the last checkpoint after your inspection, appraisal, and any repair negotiations are complete. Skipping it isn't wise — it's your chance to catch problems while you still have leverage, before ownership transfers.

If your purchase was subject to repairs handled through a contingency, the walk-through is where you verify that work actually got done.

What to Do If Something's Wrong

If something isn't right, don't panic and don't feel pressured to sign anyway. Tell your agent right away. Depending on the issue, the closing can be delayed, funds can be held back in escrow to cover a repair, or the seller can be asked to make it right before you close.

Most walk-throughs go smoothly, but the whole point is to protect you. Have questions about your walk-through or closing? Reach out and we'll help.

Frequently Asked Questions — Final Walk-Through

Is a final walk-through the same as a home inspection?

No. An inspection is a detailed professional evaluation done earlier in the process. The walk-through is a quick visual check right before closing to confirm the home's condition hasn't changed and repairs were completed.

What if the seller hasn't finished agreed-upon repairs?

Let your agent know immediately. Options include delaying closing, holding money in escrow to cover the work, or requiring the seller to complete it before the sale is finalized.

Can I skip the final walk-through?

You can, but it's rarely a good idea. It's your last chance to spot issues while you still have negotiating power, so most buyers take advantage of it.
