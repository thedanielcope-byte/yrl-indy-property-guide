What Is a Home Inspection?

Your independent look under the hood before you commit to a home.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A home inspection is your chance to learn a property's real condition before you're locked in. You hire a qualified inspector who examines the home's major systems and structure, then gives you a written report. Most Central Indiana buyers keep an inspection contingency in their offer so they can renegotiate or walk away if something serious turns up.

Who hires it
The buyer
Who pays
Usually the buyer
What it covers
Roof, structure, HVAC, electrical, plumbing

No — it's an informational report

Radon testing

What a Home Inspection Covers

A general inspection looks at the home's big-ticket systems: roof and structure, foundation, HVAC, electrical, plumbing, water heater, windows, and visible signs of moisture or pests. The inspector documents what's working, what's worn, and what may need attention soon.

It's not a pass-or-fail test and not a code inspection. Think of it as a detailed snapshot of condition so you can make an informed decision.

Why It Matters in Central Indiana

Indiana law requires most sellers to complete a residential seller's disclosure, but that reflects the seller's knowledge, not an expert's. An inspection is your own due diligence, and it often pays for itself by catching issues early.

Radon is worth a special mention: much of Central Indiana sits in a higher-radon region, so many buyers add a radon test to their inspection. It's an easy, affordable safeguard.

Using Inspection Results

Your report becomes a negotiating tool. Depending on your contract, you can ask the seller to make repairs, offer a credit, adjust the price, or, for major problems, cancel under your inspection contingency.

The goal isn't a perfect house — no home is flawless. It's making sure there are no expensive surprises you didn't sign up for.

Frequently Asked Questions — Home Inspection

Is a home inspection required in Indiana?

It isn't legally required, but it's strongly recommended and is a standard part of most purchase agreements as a contingency.

How much does a home inspection cost?

Costs vary with the home's size and age and any add-ons like radon or a sewer scope; your agent can point you to fair local pricing.

What if the inspection finds problems?

You can typically request repairs, a credit, or a price change — or, for serious issues, exercise your inspection contingency to exit the deal.
