What Is a Lot Premium?

The extra you pay for a better piece of ground beneath your new home.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A lot premium is added on top of a new home's base price when the specific homesite offers something in demand, like extra size, privacy, or a water view, similar to how upgrades and options add cost inside the home itself. Premiums vary widely by community and by how desirable that particular lot is within the plat.

Added to
The base price of the home
Common drivers
Size, shape, view, privacy, cul-de-sac location
Set by
The builder, per lot or lot type
Negotiable
Sometimes, depending on demand for that lot

What Drives a Lot Premium

Builders price lots within a community differently based on features buyers tend to value: a larger lot, a wooded or walkout-basement grade, a cul-de-sac position, or a water or greenspace view. The more in-demand the lot, the higher the premium tends to be.

Premiums are set at the community level and can change as a neighborhood sells out, since the most desirable lots are often reserved or sold first.

Weighing a Lot Premium Against the Cost

Before paying a premium, ask what specifically it buys you, extra square footage, a certain view, or simply position within the community, and compare it to what similar premiums cost at other new home communities nearby.

A premium lot can also affect resale value down the road, so it is worth thinking about the long-term appeal of that specific homesite, not just how it looks on move-in day.

Frequently Asked Questions — Lot premium

Is a lot premium included in the advertised base price?

No, base prices typically reflect the builder's standard lot. A lot premium is added separately once a specific homesite is selected.

Can lot premiums be negotiated?

Sometimes, particularly if the community has been on the market a while or the builder is motivated to sell a specific lot. It does not hurt to ask, especially alongside other pricing questions.

Do all lots in a community have a premium?

No. Many builders designate a standard lot type with no added premium, then charge extra only for specific homesites with in-demand features.

Does a lot premium affect my loan amount?

Yes, since it is part of the total purchase price, it is factored into your financing and appraisal, the same as any other cost included in the contract.
