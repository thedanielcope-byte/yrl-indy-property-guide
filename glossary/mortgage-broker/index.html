What Is a Mortgage Broker?

A go-between who shops your loan across multiple lenders instead of lending the money themselves.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A mortgage broker doesn't lend money directly; instead, they submit your application to several mortgage lenders to compare rates and programs on your behalf. This can help when your situation, such as self-employment or a lower credit score, calls for more than one option.

Role
Shops your loan to multiple lenders
Funds the loan?
No — the lender does
Paid by
Typically a fee paid by the lender or borrower
Best for
Buyers wanting to compare several loan programs

Broker vs. Direct Lender

Working with a mortgage lender directly means you're applying with one institution's own programs and pricing. A broker instead gathers your information once and shops it across a network of lenders, which can widen your options, especially for less common loan scenarios.

Because a broker isn't funding the loan, they don't carry the final underwriting decision. That authority stays with whichever lender ultimately approves and closes your loan.

When a Broker Can Help

Buyers with more complex income, credit challenges, or a need for a specialized loan program sometimes benefit most from a broker's wider access to lenders. First-time buyers who want to compare several offers side by side may also find a broker helpful.

Whichever route you choose, our agents regularly work with both brokers and direct lenders and can point you toward responsive local options in Central Indiana.

Frequently Asked Questions — Mortgage broker

Does a mortgage broker cost more than going direct to a lender?

Not necessarily. Broker compensation varies and is disclosed upfront, so it's worth comparing total costs, not just the interest rate.

Can a mortgage broker guarantee loan approval?

No broker or lender can guarantee approval before underwriting reviews your full file.

Is a mortgage broker the same as a loan officer?

No. A loan officer typically works for one lender, while a broker works with multiple lenders on your behalf.

Should I use a broker or go straight to a bank?

Both can work well. It often comes down to your specific situation and how many options you want to compare.
