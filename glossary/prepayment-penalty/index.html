What Is a Prepayment Penalty?

A fee a small number of loans charge if you pay them off faster than planned.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A prepayment penalty charges you a fee for paying your mortgage off early, whether through a home sale or a refinance. Most conventional mortgages issued today don't include one, but it's still worth checking your loan estimate and closing documents to be sure.

What it is
A fee for paying off a loan early
How common today
Rare on standard conventional mortgages
Where to check
Your loan estimate and closing disclosure
Triggered by
Selling, refinancing, or large extra payments

Why Prepayment Penalties Exist

Lenders earn money over the life of a loan through interest, so paying it off early reduces that expected income. A prepayment penalty offsets some of that loss on the small share of loans that still include one.

Modern lending rules have made prepayment penalties far less common than they once were, and many loan programs prohibit them outright.

How to Check Your Loan

Your loan estimate and closing disclosure will both disclose whether your loan includes a prepayment penalty, so review those documents closely before signing. If you plan to sell or refinance within the first few years, this is worth confirming upfront.

If you're unsure whether your current mortgage has one, your loan servicer or the original closing paperwork can confirm it.

Frequently Asked Questions — Prepayment penalty

Are prepayment penalties legal in Indiana?

Prepayment penalty rules depend on the loan type and federal regulations; many modern mortgage programs prohibit them, though it's still worth verifying your specific loan.

Do FHA or VA loans have prepayment penalties?

Government-backed loans like FHA and VA generally do not allow prepayment penalties.

How do I know if my loan has one?

Check your loan estimate and closing disclosure, or ask your lender or servicer directly.

Does selling my home early trigger a prepayment penalty?

It can, if your loan includes one and you sell within the penalty period. Most current loans do not have this feature.
