What Is Price Per Square Foot?

A quick way to compare homes on paper, useful as a starting point but not the whole story.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Price per square foot is calculated by dividing the sale or list price by the home's finished square footage. It's a useful starting point for comparing similar homes, but it can be misleading across different home styles, lot sizes, or condition, which is why agents rely on a full comparative market analysis instead.

Formula
Price divided by finished square footage
Best used for
Quick, rough comparisons
Limitation
Ignores lot, condition, upgrades, and location
Better tool for pricing
A comparative market analysis

Where It's Useful

Price per square foot works reasonably well when you're comparing similar homes in the same neighborhood, built around the same era, with similar finishes. It gives you a quick, apples-to-apples starting number without digging into a full analysis.

It's also a common shorthand agents and appraisers use in casual conversation about a market, even though it's rarely the final word on value.

Where It Falls Short

A renovated ranch and a dated two-story can carry very similar price-per-square-foot numbers while being worth very different amounts to an actual buyer. Lot size, garage, basement finish, school district, and condition all affect value in ways this single number doesn't capture.

For that reason, Your Realty Link agents use price per square foot only as one input alongside a full comparative market analysis when estimating a home's true market value.

Frequently Asked Questions — Price per square foot

Does price per square foot include unfinished basement space?

Usually not. Most agents and appraisers count only finished, livable square footage.

Can I use price per square foot to price my own home?

It can be a helpful sanity check, but it shouldn't replace a full comparative market analysis from a local agent.

Why do price-per-square-foot numbers vary so much between neighborhoods?

Location, school district, lot size, and home age all shift what buyers are willing to pay, even for similar square footage.
