What Is Proof of Funds?

The paperwork that shows a seller your offer isn't just words.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Sellers, especially in competitive situations, often want more than your word that you can close. Proof of funds is the document that backs it up: recent account statements or a lender letter showing the money is really there. On a cash offer, it usually replaces the loan approval a financed buyer would provide.

Typical form
Recent bank or brokerage statement, or lender letter
Who asks for it
Sellers and listing agents, often before accepting an offer
Most important for
Cash offers and competitive multiple-offer situations
How current
Usually within the last 30 to 60 days

When You'll Need It

Proof of funds is standard for any all-cash offer, since there's no lender approval to lean on. It's also increasingly requested alongside financed offers, both to show your down payment is real and to strengthen your offer in a competitive market.

Investors making investment offers are asked for it especially often, since sellers want confidence the deal will actually close.

What It Typically Shows

Most documentation is a recent statement from a bank, brokerage, or retirement account showing a balance that covers the purchase, sometimes combined with a letter from your bank or lender confirming the funds.

Sensitive details like full account numbers are usually redacted; the goal is simply to confirm the money exists and is accessible, not to share your full financial history.

Frequently Asked Questions — Proof of funds

Is proof of funds the same as a pre-approval letter?

No, a pre-approval letter is from a lender about a loan; proof of funds shows actual cash available, whether or not a loan is involved.

How recent does my documentation need to be?

Sellers typically want something dated within the last 30 to 60 days, though exact expectations can vary by offer.

Do I need proof of funds for a financed purchase?

Often yes, at least for your down payment and closing costs, alongside your loan pre-approval.

Can I redact information before sharing it?

Yes, it's standard to black out account numbers and unrelated balances, showing only what's needed to confirm the funds.
