What Is an HOA Reserve Fund?

The HOA's savings account for the big repairs that regular dues alone cannot cover.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

A portion of every owner's HOA dues typically goes into the reserve fund, building up savings for expenses that are predictable but not annual, like repaving a shared road. A well-funded reserve reduces the odds of a surprise special assessment, so it's one of the first things to check before buying into a community.

Funded by
A portion of regular HOA dues
Purpose
Future major repairs and replacements

Ask for the HOA's reserve study or financials
Underfunded risk
Higher chance of a special assessment

Why Reserve Funds Matter

Every shared structure and amenity eventually needs major work, whether that's a new roof, repaved streets, or pool equipment. A reserve fund lets an HOA plan and save for those costs gradually instead of hitting owners with a large bill all at once.

Associations that skip or underfund their reserve often end up relying on special assessments when something big finally breaks, which can catch new owners off guard.

Checking a Reserve Fund Before You Buy

Before buying in an HOA community, ask for the association's reserve study, a document that estimates the remaining life and replacement cost of major shared components, and compares that to what's currently saved.

A reserve funded well below the recommended level is a signal to ask more questions, since it may mean higher dues or a special assessment is on the horizon.

Frequently Asked Questions — Reserve fund

How much should be in an HOA reserve fund?

There is no single number, since it depends on the community's age, amenities, and upcoming repair needs, which is why a reserve study matters more than a dollar figure alone.

Can I see the reserve fund balance before buying?

Yes, this information is typically part of the HOA's financials or resale certificate, which your agent can request during the sale.

What happens if the reserve fund runs low?

The HOA may raise dues, levy a special assessment, or delay needed repairs, none of which are ideal for owners.

Is a reserve fund the same in every HOA?

No, funding levels and how contributions are calculated vary significantly from one association to another.
