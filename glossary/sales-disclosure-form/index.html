What Is a Sales Disclosure Form?

A routine Indiana filing that reports your sale to the county, usually handled quietly as part of closing.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Indiana requires most property sales to be reported to the county through a sales disclosure form, which helps the county assessor keep assessed value data current. This is typically prepared and filed as part of closing, so most buyers and sellers barely notice it happening.

Filed with
The county auditor's office
Reports
Sale price and transaction details
Who handles it
Usually the title company or closing agent
Purpose
Helps counties keep assessed values current

Why Indiana Requires This Filing

Counties rely on actual sale prices to keep their assessment records reasonably aligned with real market activity. Without a steady flow of reported sales, assessed values could drift further from reality over time, which is part of why this form exists.

It's required for most property transfers, though certain transaction types have exemptions. Your title company or closing agent typically handles the filing as a routine part of the closing process, not something buyers or sellers need to track down separately.

What It Means for Buyers and Sellers

For most transactions, there's little for buyers or sellers to actively do beyond providing accurate information; the form is prepared and filed alongside other closing paperwork. It's one of several documents that quietly keep the county's property records accurate after a sale.

Because it feeds directly into assessed value calculations, an accurately filed sales disclosure form is part of what keeps future property tax assessments grounded in real transaction data, rather than outdated estimates.

Frequently Asked Questions — Sales Disclosure Form

Do I need to personally file the sales disclosure form?

No, this is typically handled by the title company or closing agent as part of the standard closing process.

Is the sales disclosure form public record?

Generally yes, since it's filed with the county auditor along with other property transfer information.

Does every property sale require this form?

Most standard sales require it, though certain transfer types have exemptions; your title company can confirm for your specific transaction.

How does this form affect my property taxes?

It reports your sale price to the county, which helps inform future assessed value updates for the property.
