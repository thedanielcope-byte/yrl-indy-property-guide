What Is a Special Assessment?

The bill that shows up when an HOA's regular dues are not enough to cover a big repair.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

When an HOA faces a large expense its reserve fund cannot fully cover, it can levy a special assessment, a one-time charge on top of normal dues, split among owners. Before buying into any HOA community, ask whether a special assessment is pending or recently passed, since it can mean an unexpected bill soon after closing.

Type of charge
One-time, on top of regular dues
Common triggers
Roof, roads, major repairs, insurance shortfalls
Who pays
All owners, usually split evenly or by unit size
Best defense
A well-funded HOA reserve fund

Why HOAs Levy Special Assessments

Regular HOA dues are meant to cover routine maintenance and build up a reserve fund for future repairs. When a major expense comes up faster than the reserve can absorb, such as storm damage, an aging roof, or an insurance premium spike, the association can vote to levy a special assessment to close the gap.

A community with a healthy reserve fund is generally less likely to need frequent special assessments, which is one reason reviewing HOA financials matters before you buy.

What to Ask Before You Buy

Ask for the HOA's meeting minutes, reserve study, and any notice of a pending or recent special assessment. This information is often included in the community's resale certificate, which sellers typically provide during the sale.

If a special assessment is already approved, find out whether it's a one-time payment or spread over installments, and whether it's the buyer's or seller's responsibility at closing.

Frequently Asked Questions — Special assessment

Is a special assessment the same as regular HOA dues?

No, regular dues are ongoing, while a special assessment is a separate, one-time charge for a specific major expense.

Who pays a special assessment, the buyer or the seller?

It depends on the timing and the purchase agreement, so this is worth clarifying and putting in writing before closing.

How much can a special assessment cost?

The amount varies widely depending on the project and how it's split among owners, which is why reviewing HOA documents before buying matters.

Can I refuse to pay a special assessment?

Generally no, once approved by the association it becomes a binding obligation, and unpaid amounts can result in a lien.
