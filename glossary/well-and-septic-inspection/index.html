What Is a Well and Septic Inspection?

Essential due diligence for the many Central Indiana properties that sit outside municipal water and sewer lines.

📘 Real Estate Term 📍 Indiana 🔑 Buyers & Sellers

Quick Answer

Well and septic systems are common on homes near the edges of Central Indiana counties, where city utilities don't reach. Because these systems aren't covered by a standard home inspection, buyers typically order a separate well and septic inspection during the inspection period.

Well test covers
Water quality, flow rate, pump function
Septic test covers
Tank condition, drain field function, capacity
Who orders it
Usually the buyer
Common on
Rural and unincorporated county properties

Why These Systems Need Their Own Inspection

A general home inspector typically doesn't test well water quality or evaluate a septic system's function, since both require specialized equipment and training. A separate inspector tests the well for water quality and flow, and evaluates the septic tank and drain field for signs of failure.

Repairs to either system can be significant, so catching problems before closing matters more here than with most other inspection items.

What to Expect During the Inspection

The well inspection usually involves a water sample sent to a lab for quality testing, along with a check of flow rate and pump condition. The septic inspection typically includes pumping or opening the tank to check its condition, along with an evaluation of how well the drain field is handling wastewater.

If issues turn up, buyers commonly negotiate repairs or a credit, similar to how they'd handle findings from a standard home inspection.

Frequently Asked Questions — Well and Septic Inspection

Is a well and septic inspection required in Indiana?

It's not legally required for most sales, but lenders and buyers commonly require or strongly recommend it for properties using private systems.

How much does it cost?

It varies by provider and property, and is typically ordered alongside or shortly after the general home inspection.

What if the septic system fails inspection?

Buyers and sellers usually negotiate repairs, replacement, or a price adjustment, similar to other inspection findings.

Do all rural Central Indiana homes have well and septic systems?

Many do, especially near county edges, but always confirm rather than assume, since some rural areas are served by rural water utilities.
