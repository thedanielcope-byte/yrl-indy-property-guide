The Home Buyer’s Closing Checklist

Closing is the last — and biggest — step of buying your home. This interactive checklist walks you through what to do before closing, what to bring to the table, and how to close with confidence.

Quick Answer

Before closing, review your Closing Disclosure the moment it arrives and compare it to your Loan Estimate. On closing day, bring a government photo ID, a cashier’s check or verified wire for your cash to close, and your Closing Disclosure. Never trust wiring changes sent by email — verify by phone. A couple of weeks out: get your ducks in a row

A smooth closing starts well before closing day. Knock these out early so nothing surprises you at the table.

  • In Indiana, closings are usually handled by a title or settlement company. We’ll make sure everyone is on the same page.

  • Your lender needs proof of insurance before they’ll fund the loan. Shop a couple of quotes — premiums vary.

  • Your last chance to confirm the home’s condition, that any agreed repairs were done, and that nothing changed since your offer.

  • Until you have the keys, don’t open new credit, finance a car, make large deposits, or change jobs — any of it can derail your approval.

  • You’ll typically bring a cashier’s check or send a wire. Get the exact amount and instructions from the title company.

Three days before: your Closing Disclosure

By law, your lender must give you the Closing Disclosure at least three business days before closing. That window is for you — use it.

  • This is the final accounting of your loan and costs. Read every line.

  • Check that the loan type, interest rate, monthly payment, and cash to close match what you expected. Only certain costs are allowed to change.

  • Fees you don’t recognize, a different rate, escrow questions — get answers before you sign, not at the table.

  • So your cashier’s check or wire is for the exact amount.

⚠ Protect your money from wire fraud

Real estate closings are a top target for scammers. Never trust wiring instructions — or last-minute changes to them — that arrive by email or text. Before you move a single dollar, call the title company using a phone number you already know (not one from the email) and confirm the account details out loud. When something feels off, slow down and check with us.

Closing day: what to bring

Show up ready. Here’s your grab-list for the table.

  • A valid driver’s license, state ID, or passport.

  • Made out per the title company’s instructions.

  • To compare against the final documents one more time.

  • Everyone on the loan needs to sign.

  • For any small last-minute difference.

At the table: before you sign

Take your time. You have every right to read and understand what you’re signing, no matter how long it takes.

  • Loan amount, interest rate, and monthly payment should be identical on the note, the mortgage, and your Closing Disclosure.

  • Are they escrowed into your monthly payment, or do you pay them yourself? Indiana property taxes are billed in two installments each year.

  • Know who will service your loan and how HOA payments work if your neighborhood has an association.

  • Make sure you leave the table with everything you need to get into your home.

Buyer’s Closing Checklist FAQs

When do I get my Closing Disclosure?

By law, at least three business days before closing. That window exists so you can review the final loan terms and costs without pressure — use it, and bring any questions to your loan officer before closing day.

What do I need to bring to closing in Indiana?

A government photo ID for everyone on the loan, a cashier's check or proof of a verified wire for your cash to close, a copy of your Closing Disclosure, and your co-borrower if you have one. We'll give you the exact amount and instructions ahead of time.

How much money will I need at closing?

Your "cash to close" is your down payment plus closing costs, minus credits and your earnest money. The exact figure is on your Closing Disclosure. See our buyer closing costs guide for what’s included.

Can I still walk away at closing?

Until you sign, yes — though there can be consequences depending on your contract and contingencies. If something feels wrong, tell us and pause. It’s always better to ask than to sign a deal you’re not comfortable with.

Who handles the closing?

In Central Indiana, closings are typically conducted by a title or settlement company that also issues title insurance. Your Realty Link coordinates with them, your lender, and the other side so closing day goes smoothly.
