An open house is one of the oldest, simplest ways to see a home — and in 2026 it comes in two forms. There's the traditional open house: a listed home opened to the public for a few hours, usually on a weekend, where anyone can walk through at their own pace. And there's the virtual open house: a tour you take remotely, without driving across town or flying across the country. Your Realty Link does both. We host in-person open houses for our sellers, and for buyers who can't be there — relocating, out of state, or just short on time — we offer private, live-video showings of any home on the market. This guide walks through how each one works, the honest pros and cons, and how to use them to actually find (or sell) the right home.

What Is an Open House?

A traditional open house is a scheduled window — often a two- or three-hour block on a Saturday or Sunday — when a listed home is open for anyone to tour without an appointment. The listing agent (or a host agent) is there to greet visitors, answer questions, and share information about the property. It's a low-pressure way to see homes, compare neighborhoods, and get a feel for what your budget buys in a given area. A virtual open house serves the same purpose — seeing the home and getting your questions answered — but does it over video so you can attend from anywhere.

Traditional (In-Person) Open Houses

There's still nothing quite like standing in a home. You feel the flow of the rooms, the ceiling height, the natural light, the sounds of the street, and whether the layout actually works for how you live. In-person open houses are especially useful for local buyers who are early in the search and want to tour several homes and neighborhoods in an afternoon.

A few things to know before you go: the agent hosting the open house represents the seller, not you, so be friendly but measured about what you share. Look past the staging — check water pressure, open closets, note smells, and pay attention to the roof, windows, and mechanicals. And if you're seriously shopping, it's worth having your own buyer's agent lined up before you start signing in at open houses.

Virtual Open Houses — Tour From Anywhere

A virtual open house lets you see a home without being in the room. Across the industry, "virtual" can mean a few different things — a live broadcast on social media, a pre-recorded walkthrough video, or a self-guided 3D tour you click through yourself. Each has its place, but they all share one limitation: you can't ask a question and get an answer in the moment, or say "go back and show me that water stain again."

That's why Your Realty Link's virtual open houses are private, live-video showings. You pick a time, and one of our agents tours the home for you on a live call over FaceTime, Zoom, or Google Meet. You direct where the camera goes, ask anything you'd ask in person, and get a straight answer on the spot. It's a real showing — just with our feet on the ground and your eyes on the screen.

In modern times, this is genuinely powerful. It's how out-of-state buyers relocating to Indianapolis tour ten homes in a week without booking a flight. It's how busy professionals shortlist homes on a lunch break. And it's how smart local buyers do a quick first pass before spending a Saturday driving to homes that photos made look better than they are. Virtual isn't a lesser option — for a lot of people, it's the better first step.

Traditional vs. Virtual: Honest Pros & Cons

Neither one is "better" — they're better for different moments. Here's the straight comparison so you can pick the right tool.

Traditional Open House

Best for: local buyers, getting a true feel for space and neighborhood.

  • Pro: You feel the real flow, light, and scale
  • Pro: Great for comparing several homes in an area
  • Pro: Spontaneous — no appointment needed
  • Con: Fixed day and time; you have to be there
  • Con: Weather, travel, and crowds are all factors

Virtual (Live-Video) Showing

Best for: out-of-town buyers, busy schedules, first-pass filtering.

  • Pro: Tour from anywhere, on your schedule
  • Pro: A real agent answers your questions live
  • Pro: Shortlist fast before touring in person
  • Con: You can't physically feel the space
  • Con: Needs a decent connection on both ends

The best strategy for most buyers is to use them together: a quick live-video showing to decide whether a home is worth a trip, then an in-person visit to confirm the ones that make the cut.

For Sellers: Do Open Houses Actually Sell Homes?

Here's the honest answer: a single open house rarely sells the house by itself. Most buyers who show up are early in their search, and some are neighbors. But open houses — traditional and virtual — are still valuable, because they widen the pool of people who experience your home and create momentum in the critical first days on market. What actually sells a home is the whole package: correct pricing, strong staging and photography, full MIBOR MLS exposure, and an agent who markets it everywhere buyers are looking.

For Buyers: How to Use Open Houses Wisely

  • Line up your own agent first. The host works for the seller. A buyer's agent represents you — and it typically costs you nothing to have one.
  • Use virtual to filter, in-person to confirm. A quick live-video showing saves you weekends. Reserve in-person trips for the real contenders.
  • Look past the staging. Check the mechanicals, the roofline, storage, and the neighborhood at different times of day.
  • Come prepared. Get pre-approved before you're serious, so you can move quickly when you find the one.
  • New to the area? Our relocation buyers lean on live-video showings to tour before they ever land.

Frequently Asked Questions — Open Houses

What is the difference between a traditional and a virtual open house?

A traditional open house is an in-person event where anyone can walk through a listed home during a set window, usually on a weekend. A virtual open house lets you tour a home remotely. At Your Realty Link, our virtual open houses are private, live-video tours: an agent walks through the home in real time over FaceTime, Zoom, or Google Meet, points the camera wherever you want, and answers your questions live.

How do Your Realty Link virtual open houses work?

You pick a time, we schedule a private live-video showing, and one of our agents tours the home for you on a live call. You direct where the camera goes, ask questions as you would in person, and get an honest read on the home — all from wherever you are. It's ideal for out-of-town buyers, busy schedules, and narrowing a list before you drive out in person.

Where can I find open houses in Indianapolis?

You can browse upcoming open houses across Central Indiana on MIBOR's official public property search, which lists in-person open houses posted by member brokerages. Your Realty Link can also set you up with alerts for open houses that match your search and arrange a private live-video tour of any home you want to see remotely.

Do I need my own agent to attend an open house?

You don't need an agent to walk through a public open house, but it's smart to have one representing you before you tour seriously or sign in. The agent hosting an open house works for the seller. Having your own buyer's agent means someone is looking out for your interests on price, condition, and terms. Your Realty Link offers buyer representation across Central Indiana.
