Our team at Your Realty Link helps personal representatives across Indianapolis and Central Indiana price, prepare and sell estate homes, working alongside your attorney so the sale fits the probate process. We’ll be patient, clear and organized, and we’ll keep every heir informed.

Note: We are real estate professionals, not probate attorneys. Everything below is general information about Indiana law; your estate attorney is the right person to advise on your situation.

How Probate Home Sales Work in Indiana

The court appoints a personal representative (an executor if there’s a will, an administrator if there isn’t) and issues “letters” that prove that person’s authority. The personal representative takes possession of the estate’s real estate, keeps paying the taxes, keeps the home in repair, and may insure it (IC 29-1-13-1). The personal representative, not the individual heirs, signs the listing and the deed. How the sale works depends on whether the estate is supervised or unsupervised.

Supervised vs. Unsupervised Administration

Unsupervised administration is available when the heirs or beneficiaries agree, the estate is solvent, and the court finds the personal representative qualified. A will can also authorize it. In an unsupervised estate, the personal representative can sell real estate at a public or private sale without a separate court order (IC 29-1-7.5-2, IC 29-1-7.5-3).

Supervised administration keeps the court more involved. Unless the will grants a power of sale, the personal representative petitions the court and gives notice to heirs and lienholders, and the court issues an order to sell. In a private sale, the price can’t be below the fair market value on record, and the court may require an appraisal (IC 29-1-15-2, IC 29-1-15-11, IC 29-1-15-13, IC 29-1-15-14). We build that court step into the listing plan from day one.

Probate Home Sale Timeline

  • Opening the estate: don’t wait. Indiana limits selling a decedent’s real estate to pay certain debts if the estate isn’t opened within about five months of death (IC 29-1-7-15.1).
  • Notice to creditors: published once a week for two weeks after letters are issued (IC 29-1-7-7).
  • Claims window: generally three months from the first published notice, and claims are barred nine months after death (IC 29-1-14-1).
  • Closing the estate: estates are expected to close within about a year of appointment unless there’s good cause (IC 29-1-16-2, IC 29-1-7.5-3.8).

Your attorney decides when it makes sense to list. Time on the market depends on the home, its condition and the season.

Probate Home Sale Questions

Can I sell the house before probate is finished?

Often, yes. Once you have letters, a personal representative in an unsupervised estate can sell without a separate court order, and a supervised estate can sell after the court approves it. Your attorney will advise you on timing around creditor claims.

Do I need court approval to sell a house in an Indiana probate estate?

In a supervised estate, generally yes, unless the will grants a power of sale. In an unsupervised estate, no separate court order is required to sell real estate.

Do we have to fix up the house first?

No. Many estate homes sell as-is. Indiana's seller's disclosure form statute doesn't apply to sales by a personal representative administering an estate (IC 32-21-5-1), though other disclosure rules may still apply, so ask the estate's attorney.

How long does a probate home sale take?

Creditors generally have three months from the first published notice to file claims, and estates are expected to close within about a year of appointment. Supervised estates add a court step. We'll map a realistic timeline with you and your attorney.

Where are probate cases handled in Marion County?

Estate administration in Marion County is heard in Marion Superior Court, Court D08, at the Community Justice Campus, 675 Justice Way, Indianapolis.
